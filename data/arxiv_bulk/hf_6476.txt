1  Multi-scale Spectrum Sensing in 5G Cognitive Networks  arXiv:1802.08378v1 [cs.IT] 23 Feb 2018  Nicolo Michelusi, Matthew Nokleby, Urbashi Mitra, and Robert Calderbank  Abstract A multi-scale approach to spectrum sensing is proposed to overcome the huge energy cost of acquiring full network state information over 5G cognitive networks. Secondary users (SUs) estimate the local spectrum occupancies and aggregate them hierarchically to produce multi-scale estimates. Thus, SUs obtain fine-grained estimates of spectrum occupancies of nearby cells, more relevant to resource allocation tasks, and coarse-grained estimates of those of distant cells. The proposed architecture accounts for local estimation errors, delays in the exchange of state information, as well as irregular interference patterns arising in future fifth-generation (5G) dense cellular systems with irregular cell deployments. An algorithm based on agglomerative clustering is proposed to design an energy-efficient aggregation hierarchy matched to the irregular interference structure, resilient to aggregation delays and local estimation errors. Measuring performance in terms of the trade-off between SU network throughput, interference to PUs and energy efficiency, numerical experiments demonstrate a 10% improvement in SU network throughput over a regular tree construction, for a reference value of interference to PUs, and up to 1/4th of the energy cost needed to acquire full network state information. Cognitive radio [6] has the potential to improve spectral efficiency by enabling smart terminals (secondary users, SUs) to exploit resource gaps left by primary users (PUs) [7]. However, estimating these resource gaps in real-time becomes increasingly challenging with the increasing network densification, due to the signaling overhead required to learn the network state. Furthermore, network densification results in irregular network topologies. Such irregularities are exacerbated by the expected increasing reliance of 5G systems on millimeter-wave (mm-wave) transmission to alleviate the spectrum crunch. Although initially expected to be noise-limited, densely deployed mm-wave networks have been shown to be interference-limited [8], and thus require careful management of interference. making interference management an important research question. Further, channel propagation at these frequencies is sensitive to blockages [9], [10]. The combination of densely deployed networks (at all frequencies) and blockages (specific to mm-wave) result in irregular interference patterns, which demand effective interference management. To meet this challenge, we develop and analyze spectrum utilization and interference management techniques for cognitive radios with irregular interference patterns, including dense and mm-wave deployments. The framework developed in this paper can be applied, for instance, to the design of spectrum sharing systems among multiple operators operating at mm-wave bands via secondary licensing [11]: therein, the secondary network must operate by guaranteeing a maximum interference constraint to the primary network. We suppose a cellular network with a set of PUs, licensed to access the spectrum, and a set of opportunistic SUs, which seek access to unoccupied spectrum. The SUs must estimate the channel occupancy of the PU from local measurements. In principle, these measurements can be collected at a fusion center, but centralized estimation is expensive in practice and may incur unacceptable delays [12]. To reduce this cost, neighboring cells may inform each other of resources they are occupying, to provide a form of coordination [13], however this scheme is not capable of managing interference extending beyond the cell neighborhood, which may be relevant in dense topologies. Instead, we design an energy efficient multi-scale solution which exploits the structure and irregularities of interference over the network and is resilient to delays and errors in the information exchange and estimation processes. Local measurements are fused up a tree, which provides aggregate spectrum occupancy information for collections of cells. This solution ensures that SUs have precise information on the spectrum occupancies of nearby cells, and coarse, aggregate  February 26, 2018  DRAFT  3  Fig. 1: System model (see notation in Sec. III). information on the occupancies of faraway cells. It permits an efficient trade-off of estimation quality, cost of aggregation, and estimation delay. We derive the ideal estimator of the global spectrum occupancy from the multi-scale measurements, and further derive the SU spetrum use strategies that maximize the long-term reward of the network. To tailor the measurement tree to the interference pattern of the network, we design an agglomerative clustering algorithm [14, Ch. 14]. We measure the end-to-end performance in terms of the trade-off among SU network throughput, interference to PUs, and the energy efficiency of aggregation. While the proposed framework applies to both sub-6GHz and mmwave 5G networks, we provide numerical results for a dense mm-wave network. We show that our design outperforms a regular tree construction proposed in [1] by 10%, and requires as little as 1/4th the energy cost of acquiring full network state information at a fusion center. Hierarchical estimation was proposed in [15] in the context of averaging consensus [16], which is a prototype for distributed, linear estimation schemes. Consensus-based schemes for spectrum estimation have also been proposed in [17], [18]. In contrast, we focus on a dynamic setting. The ability to adapt to time-varying network dynamics is especially important in mm-wave networks, which have been shown to be highly susceptible to mobility of users and blockages [19]. A framework for joint spectrum sensing and scheduling in wireless networks has been proposed in [20], for the case of a single cell. Instead, we consider a multi-cell network. Lowcost organization of femtocell overlay networks waas considered in [21], whereas scheduling device-to-device communications in small, mm-wave cells is considered in [22]. To summarize, the contributions of this paper are as follows. Hierarchical information exchange over the tree In the previous section, we discussed the local estimation at the cell level. We now describe the hierarchical fusion of local estimates to collect NSI. This fusion is patterned after hierarchical averaging, a technique for scalar average consensus in wireless networks developed in [15]. The cell head, at the beginning of the SU data communication phase within frame t, has a local spectrum estimate b̂i,t , representing the probability that bi,t = 1, given local spectrum measurements collected up to time t. These local estimates are fused up the hierarchy, incurring a delay. Let δL,i ≥ 0 be the delay incurred to propagate the spectrum estimate of cell i all the way up to its level-L cluster head HL (i). It includes the local processing time at each intermediate level-L0 cluster head traversed before reaching the level-L cluster head, where L0 < L, as well as the delay to traverse the links (possibly, multi-hop) connecting successive cluster heads. We assume that δL,i is an integer, multiple of the frame duration; in fact, scheduling of SUs transmissions in the data communication phase is done immediately after spectrum sensing, hence a spectrum estimate with non-integer delay δL,i can only be used for scheduling decisions with delay dδL,i e. Optimizing the network utility over the set of all possible trees is a combinatorial problem with high complexity. Thus, we use agglomerative hierarchical clustering, developed in [14, Ch. 14], in which a tree is built by successively combining smaller clusters based on a similarity metric. N UMERICAL R ESULTS In this section, we provide numerical results. We use an interference model motivated by emerging 5G cellular systems. Nodes transmit and receive in the mm-wave band and employ highly-directional antenna arrays [26], [27]. Instead of reflecting off of objects as in standard cellular networks, mm-wave transmissions tend to be absorbed by objects, resulting in blockage [28] that severely attenuates the wireless signal. We adopt a model with stochastic blockage and sectored beamforming [29], [30]: rectangular blockages of fixed height and width are placed randomly on the boundaries between cells. We say that links between cells i, j are line of sight (LOS) if the line segment connecting the centers of cells i and j does not intersect any blockage object. Otherwise, such links are said to be non-LOS (NLOS). Accordingly, we define LOS and NLOS path loss exponents, denoted αL and αN , respectively. We use a sectored beamforming model as in [31].
