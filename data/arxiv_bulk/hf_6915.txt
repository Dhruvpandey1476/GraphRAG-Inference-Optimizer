A GARDEN OF EDEN THEOREM FOR PRINCIPAL ALGEBRAIC ACTIONS  arXiv:1706.06548v1 [math.DS] 20 Jun 2017  TULLIO CECCHERINI-SILBERSTEIN AND MICHEL COORNAERT Abstract. Let Γ be a countable abelian group and f ∈ Z[Γ], where Z[Γ] denotes the integral group ring of Γ. Consider the Pontryagin dual Xf of the cyclic Z[Γ]-module Z[Γ]/Z[Γ]f and suppose that the natural action of Γ on Xf is expansive and that Xf is connected. We prove that if τ : Xf → Xf is a Γ-equivariant continuous map, then τ is surjective if and only if the restriction of τ to each Γ-homoclinicity class is injective. This is an analogue of the classical Garden of Eden theorem of Moore and Myhill for cellular automata with finite alphabet over Γ.  1. Introduction Consider a dynamical system (X, α), consisting of a compact metrizable space X, called the phase space, equipped with a continuous action α of a countable group Γ. Let d be a metric on X that is compatible with the topology. Two points x, y ∈ X are said to be homoclinic if limγ→∞ d(γx, γy) = 0, i.e., for every ε > 0, there exists a finite set F ⊂ Γ such that d(γx, γy) < ε for all γ ∈ Γ \ F . Homoclinicity is an equivalence relation on X. By compactness of X, this relation does not depend of the choice of the compatible metric d. A map with source set X is called pre-injective (with respect to α) if its restriction to each homoclinicity class is injective. An endomorphism of the dynamical system (X, α) is a continuous map τ : X → X that is Γ-equivariant (i.e., τ (γx) = γτ (x) for all γ ∈ Γ and x ∈ X). The original Garden of Eden theorem is a statement in symbolic dynamics that characterizes surjective endomorphisms of shift systems with finite alphabet. To be more specific, let us fix a compact metrizable space A, called the alphabet. Given a countable group Γ, the shift over the group Γ with alphabet A is the dynamical system (AΓ , σ), where AΓ = {x : Γ → A} is equipped with the product topology and σ is the action defined by γx(γ ′ ) := x(γ −1 γ ′ ) for all x ∈ AΓ and γ, γ ′ ∈ Γ. It was first proved for Γ = Zd by Moore and Myhill in the early 1960s. Actually, the implication surjective =⇒ pre-injective was first proved by Moore in [13] while the converse implication was established shortly after by Myhill in [15]. The Garden of Eden theorem was subsequently extended to finitely generated groups of subexponential growth by Machı̀ and Mignosi [12] and finally to all countable amenable groups by Machı̀, Scarabotti, and the first author in [7]. Let us say that the dynamical system (X, α) has the Moore property if every surjective endomorphism of (X, α) is pre-injective and that it has the Myhill property if every pre-injective endomorphism of (X, α) is surjective. We say that the dynamical system (X, α) has the MooreMyhill property, or that it satisfies the Garden of Eden theorem, if it has both the Moore and the Myhill properties. It turns out that both the Moore and the Myhill properties for shifts with finite alphabet characterize amenable groups. Indeed, if Γ is non-amenable, on one hand, Bartholdi [1] proved the existence of a finite alphabet A such that (AΓ , σ) does not have the Moore property, and, on the other, Bartholdi and Kielak [2] proved the existence of a finite alphabet B such that (B Γ , σ) does not have the Myhill property. The dynamical system (X, α) is called expansive if there exists a constant δ > 0 such that, for every pair of distinct points x, y ∈ X, there exists an element γ ∈ Γ such that d(γx, γy) ≥ δ. Such a constant δ is called an expansiveness constant for (X, α, d). The fact that (X, α) is expansive or not does not depend on the choice of the metric d. For instance, the shift system (AΓ , σ) is expansive for every countable group Γ whenever the alphabet A is finite. The goal of the present paper is to establish a version of the Garden of Eden theorem for expansive principal algebraic systems with connected phase space over countable abelian groups. By an algebraic dynamical system, we mean a dynamical system of the form (X, α), where X is a compact metrizable abelian group and α is an action of a countable group Γ on X by continuous group automorphisms. By Pontryagin duality, algebraic dynamical systems with acting group Γ are in one-to-one correspondence with countable left Z[Γ]-modules. Here Z[Γ] denotes the integral group ring of Γ. This correspondence has been intensively studied in the last decades and revealed fascinating connections between commutative algebra, number theory, harmonic analysis, ergodic theory, and dynamical systems (see in particular the monograph [16] and the survey [11]). Let f ∈ Z[Γ] and consider the cyclic left Z[Γ]-module Mf := Z[Γ]/Z[Γ]f obtained by quotienting the ring Z[Γ] by the principal left ideal generated by f . The algebraic dynamical system associated by Pontryagin  A GARDEN OF EDEN THEOREM  3  duality with Mf is denoted by (Xf , αf ) and is called the principal algebraic dynamical system associated with f . Our main result is the following. Theorem 1.1. Let Γ be a countable abelian group (e.g. Γ = Zd ) and f ∈ Z[Γ]. Suppose that the principal algebraic dynamical system (Xf , αf ) associated with f is expansive and that Xf is connected. Then the dynamical system (Xf , αf ) has the Moore-Myhill property. There are two main ingredients in our proof of this theorem. The first one is a rigidity result of Bhattacharya [3] for expansive algebraic systems with connected phase space. We use it to prove that every endomorphism of (Xf , αf ) is affine with linear part of the form x 7→ rx for some r ∈ Z[Γ]. The second one is a result of Lind and Schmidt [10] which asserts that the homoclinic group of (Xf , αf ) (i.e., the subgroup of X consisting of all points homoclinic to 0X ), equipped with the discrete topology and the induced action of Γ, is conjugate to the Pontryagin dual Z[Γ]/Z[Γ]f of Xf . Our motivation for the present work originated from a sentence of Gromov [9, p. 195] suggesting that the Garden of Eden theorem could be extended to dynamical systems with a suitable hyperbolic flavor other than shifts and subshifts. A first step in that direction was made in [6], where we proved that all Anosov diffeomorphisms on tori generate Z-actions with the Moore-Myhill property, and another one in [5], where we gave sufficient conditions for expansive actions of countable amenable groups to have the Myhill property. The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces notation and collects background material on algebraic dynamical systems. In Section 3, we discuss topological rigidity of expansive algebraic dynamical systems. The proof of Theorem 1.1 is given in Section 4. In the last section, we collect some final remarks and exhibit some examples showing that Theorem 1.1 becomes false if the expansiveness hypothesis is replaced by the weaker hypothesis that the system (Xf , αf ) is mixing. 2.
