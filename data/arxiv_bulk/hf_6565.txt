Hiding higher order cross-correlations of multivariate data using Archimedean copulas Krzysztof Domino∗1 and Adam Glos1,2  arXiv:1803.07813v1 [cs.DS] 21 Mar 2018  1  Institute of Theoretical and Applied Informatics, Polish Academy of Sciences, Bałtycka 5, 44-100 Gliwice, Poland 2 Institute of Informatics, Silesian University of Technology, ul. Akademicka 16, 44-100 Gliwice, Poland March 22, 2018  Abstract In this paper we present the algorithm that changes the subset of marginals of multivariate normal distributed data into such modelled by an Archimedean copula. Proposed algorithm leaves a correlation matrix almost unchanged, but introduces a higher order crosscorrelation measured by high order multivariate cumulant tensors. Given the algorithm, we analyse the ability of cumulants based features selection methods to detect a subset of changed data. We show numerically that the performance of the method based on a second cumulant (a covariance matrix) is weak comparing to method that uses the 3 order multivariate cumulant tensor. Our data generation algorithm can be used for hiding information in randomly distributed data or for the features discrimination algorithms comparison.  Keywords: Cumulant tensors, Archimedean copulas, features discrimination, Joint Skewness Band Selection, Maximum Ellipsoid Volume.  1  Introduction  While dealing with machine learning purposes [1] we often have n-variate data what correspond to n features. Due to high dimensionality we may need a subset of significant features. We can do it either by a features extraction, i.e. determining some combinations of original features, or by features selection, i.e. selecting some features. The main advantage of the second approach is that extracted features possess straightforward interpretation. Feature selection can be performed in the supervised or non-supervised way. The first approach requires training samples, and priory knowledge about data which is out of interest in our research. There are many methods of features selection based on different criterion such as a covariance matrix [2], correlation (features similarity) [3], information entropy [4] or information divergence [5]. In this paper we consider cumulants based methods to select a subset of random variables. Two particularly interesting method can be distinguished: MEV (Maximum Ellipsoid Volume) [2], which chooses variables according to the covariance matrix, and JSBS (Joint Skewness Band Selection) [6] that uses the 3rd order cumulant. Both methods iteratively remove the least informative variable by subtensor analysis, however first method is limited to second moment information, and hence can detect less interdependence between variables. This is not the case for JSBS algorithm, which analyses third order dependencies. ∗  kdomino@iitis.pl  1  Our main contribution is an algorithm which replace part of the multivariate normal data by samples distributed according to Archimedean copula. Our malformation generates samples with similar second-order correlation, however with additional third-order dependencies. The effect is confirmed by MEV and JSBS analysis, as the first algorithm works much worse with newly generated data than the second. The paper is organized as follows. In Sec. 2 we provides basic facts concerning multivariate distribution, including Archimedean copulas. In Sec. 3 we present and analyse our algorithm. In Sec. 4 we sum up our results and discuss its applications and extensions.  2  Preliminaries  Copulas C(u) ∈ [0, 1] is a multivariate join cumulative distribution function with uniform marginal on [0, 1] segment [7, 8], i.e. u ∈ [0, 1]n . Sub-copula Ck with given multindex k = (k1 , . . . , kp ) ⊂ (1, . . . , n) is defined as the joint cumulative distribution of subset k of marginal distributions. Cumulant tensors M d ∈ R[n,d] of order d are a multivariate extension of univariate distribution cumulants [9], which are a super-symmetric tensors [10,11] indexed by a multi-index i = (i1 , . . . id ). Note that the first cumulant is an expected value vector and the second is a covariance matrix. Hence first and second cumulant fully describes Gaussian multivariate distribution, for which cumulants of order higher than 2 are zero [12, 13]. To introduce Archimedean copulas let ψθ : [0, ∞] → [0, 1] be arbitrary continuous function such that ψθ (0) = 1 and ψθ (∞) = 0. In details for each marginal variable Vi the algorithm computes a determinant of subcovariance matrix which is constructed from the original one by removing i-th column and row. The variable, which provides smallest determinant is considered to be the least informative at the point. Then we recursively search for consecutive variable in remaining collection. While our algorithm is designed for special Archimedean families, it can be easily generalised to other Archimedean copulas. To correctness of our algorithm was numerically confirmed by comparing outputs of feature discrimination MEV and JSBS algorithms for various choice of covariance matrices. While for some covariance matrices MEV has provided almost random results, JSBS in most cases achieved full discrimination. The result can be considered in various direction. First it can be considered as a way of hiding information in randomly distributed data. Secondly we have proposed a method of data generation, which can be considered for feature discrimination algorithms comparison. The result can be generalized in various direction. First we believe our result can be improved, to reduce discriminability of MEV algorithm. Secondly it can be generalized into larger collection of multivariate distribution, not even necessarily involving Archimedean copulas.
