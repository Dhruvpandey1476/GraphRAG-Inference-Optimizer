G always denotes a group, |G| is the order of G, σ(G) is the set of all primes dividing |G|, π denotes a set of some primes. Let P be the set of all primes, then let π ′ = P \ π. We use Gπ denote a Hall π-subgroup of G. Recall that a class F of groups is called a Fitting class if F is closed under taking normal subgroups and products of normal F-subgroups. As usual, we denote by E, S, N the classes of all groups, all soluble groups, all nilpotent groups, respectively; Eπ , Sπ , Nπ denote the classes of all π-groups, all soluble π-groups, all nilpotent π-groups, respectively; and Sπ and Nπ to denote the classes of all π-soluble groups and all π-nilpotent groups, respectively. It is well known that all the above classes are Fitting classes. From the definition of Fitting class, we see that for each non-empty Fitting class F, every group G has a unique maximal normal F-subgroup, which is called the F-radical of G and denoted by GF . For any non-empty class F of groups, a subgroup V of G is said to be F-maximal if V ∈ F and U = V whenever V ≤ U ≤ G and U ∈ F.
