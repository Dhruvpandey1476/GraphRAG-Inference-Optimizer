Non-asymptotic Bayesian Minimax Adaptation  arXiv:1609.00940v4 [math.ST] 6 Mar 2018  in Continuous-Time Gaussian Channels Keisuke Yano 1 and Fumiyasu Komaki 1,2 1 Department  of Mathematical Informatics, Graduate School of Information Science and Technology, The University of Tokyo, 7-3-1 Hongo, Bunkyo-ku, Tokyo 113-8656, Japan  e-mail: yano@mist.i.u-tokyo.ac.jp; komaki@mist.i.u-tokyo.ac.jp 2 RIKEN  Brain Science Institute, 2-1 Hirosawa, Wako City, Saitama 351-0198, Japan  Abstract: This paper studies Bayesian approaches to non-asymptotic minimax adaptation in nonparametric estimation. We discuss estimating an input function on the basis of the output function from continuous-time Gaussian channels. The input function is assumed to be in a Sobolev ellipsoid with an unknown smoothness level and an unknown radius. In this problem, the minimax risk over a Sobolev ellipsoid is invariant when the ratio of the squared radius and the noise variance is unchanged. However, existing Bayes estimators that are asymptotically minimax as the noise variance goes to zero ignore this invariance. Violating invariance causes excess risks when the radius of the parameter space is large. To avoid such excess risks, we focus our attention on non-asymptotic minimax adaptation, that is, on attaining non-asymptotic minimaxity up to a universal constant without using knowledge regarding the smoothness level and the radius of the parameter space. We provide a non-asymptotic risk bound for a refined sieve prior from which we show that the Bayes estimator is non-asymptotically minimax adaptive. In addition, we also provide an application to estimation in nonparametric regression models. MSC 2010 subject classifications: Primary 62G05; secondary 62G20. Literature review There is an extensive literature on asymptotically minimax adaptation in Gaussian infinite sequence models. A pioneer study was published by Efromoivich and Pinsker [14]. An estimator based on the blockwise James–Stein estimator was shown to be asymptotically minimax adaptive in [10, 11]. From a Bayesian perspective, several studies are available. Belitser and Ghosal [6] showed that in the case in which the smoothness level α0 is included in a discrete set, putting prior distributions on the hyperparameter α in the Gaussian distribution G(· | α) leads to asymptotically minimax adaptation. Scricciolo [36] obtained a similar result in Bayesian nonparametric density estimation. Huang [24] removed the assumption on α0 , incurring the need to pay the price that a logarithmic factor is added to the rate. See also [2, 18, 27, 34]. Recently, the results on rate-exact Bayesian minimax adaptation without any assumption on α0 have become available in [16, 23, 25, 26]. These studies have focused only on asymptotically minimax adaptation under the asymptotic as ε → 0 and none of them has discussed non-asymptotic adaptation. Some of them lack non-asymptotic adaptation, as shown in Section 2. Non-asymptotic minimax adaptation has been studied from the viewpoints of model selection and frequentist model averaging. In nonparametric density estimation, Birgé and Massart [7] showed that the estimator based on an analogue of Mallows’ Cp [29] (equivalently, the Akaike Information Criterion (AIC) [1] and Stein’s Unbiased Risk Estimator (SURE) [39]) attains non-asymptotic minimax adaptation; see also [4] for more general results. For similar results in nonparametric regression models and in Gaussian infinite sequence models, see [3, 8]. Non-asymptotic minimax adaptation is also attained using frequentist model averaging. A slight modification of the arguments in [12, 28] shows that the frequentist model averaging estimator using Mallows’ Cp has non-asymptotic minimax adaptation. Although some connection between the frequentist model averaging estimator and Bayesian model averaging was discussed in [21] (see also the appendix in [28]), posterior distributions of θ are not available in the frequentist model averaging approach. For this reason, we distinguish the frequentist model averaging from a Bayesian model averaging. In Section 2, we  K. Yano and F. Komaki/Non-asymptotic Bayesian Minimax Adaptation  5  review these results in our context. Yet, the question whether there exists a fully Bayesian approach attaining non-asymptotic minimax adaptation remains unresolved. The prior distribution that we use is a modification of a sieve prior discussed in [2, 34, 45, 37, 38]. Originally, the sieve prior was introduced by Zhao [45] to resolve some Bayesian nonparametric problems regarding prior masses on the parameter space. Arbel et al. [2] showed that the Bayes estimator based on the sieve prior is asymptotically minimax adaptive up to a logarithmic factor under the asymptotics as ε → 0. A practical advantage of sieve priors in Gaussian sequence models is that an exact sampling from the posterior distributions is performed using a simple acceptance-rejection method. However, the Bayes estimator based on the original sieve prior in [45] fails non-asymptotically minimax adaptation (see Section 2) and, thus, a non-trivial refinement is necessary. 1.2. Organization The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we review several existing estimators from the viewpoint of non-asymptotic adaptation. In Section 3, a non-asymptotically adaptive Bayes estimator is proposed. This is the principal part of this study. Section 4 presents numerical experiments. Conclusions In this paper, we presented improved Bayesian nonparametric estimators in continuous-time Gaussian channel with an unknown smoothness α0 and an unknown radius B, focusing on the invariance of the minimax risk. We observed that while the minimax risk is invariant when the value of ε/B is unchanged, existing asymptotically minimax estimators such as Bayes estimators based on Gaussian priors and estimators using truncation violate this invariance. This violation leads to excess risks in large B regions. To avoid such excess risks, we constructed a Bayes estimator that is non-asymptotically adaptive. We also provide an application to constructing a non-asymptotically adaptive Bayes estimator in nonparametric regression models. We propose two principal future studies. The first study is to find a means of attaining non-asymptotic adaptation in the empirical Bayesian manner. Recently, Petrone et al. [31] and Rousseau and Szabó [35] established important asymptotic results on the performance of empirical Bayesian nonparametrics. Focusing on a simple setting, we expect to be able to answer whether there exists an empirical Bayesian method attaining non-asymptotic adaptation. The investigation would also provide insight into the relationship among Bayesian nonparametrics, empirical Bayesian nonparametrics, model selection, and frequentist model averaging. The second is to investigate non-asymptotic Bayesian adaptation in the other settings. The present paper used a Gaussian infinite sequence model under Sobolev-type parameter constraints for simplicity and for  K. Yano and F. Komaki/Non-asymptotic Bayesian Minimax Adaptation  19  clarity of presentation. One possible extension of our work would be to investigate non-asymptotic Bayesian adaptation in density estimation. Resolution of these problems will increase our understanding of nonparametric estimation.
