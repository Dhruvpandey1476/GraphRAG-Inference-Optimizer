LARGE GIRTH GRAPHS WITH BOUNDED DIAMETER-BY-GIRTH RATIO  arXiv:1803.09229v1 [math.GR] 25 Mar 2018  GOULNARA ARZHANTSEVA AND ARINDAM BISWAS Abstract. We provide an explicit construction of finite regular graphs (Γk )k∈N with girth Γk → ∞ Γk t as k → ∞ and diam girth Γk 6 D for some D > 0 and all k ∈ N. For each dimension n ∈ {2, q + 1}, where q is a prime and t ∈ N, we find a pair of matrices in SLn (Z) such that (i) they generate a free subgroup, (ii) their reduction mod p generate SLn (Fp ) for all sufficiently large primes p, (iii) the corresponding Cayley graphs of SLn (Fp ) have girth at least cn log p for some cn > 0. Relying on growth results (with no use of expansion properties of the involved graphs), we observe that the diameter of those Cayley graphs is at most O(log p). This gives infinite sequences of finite 4-regular Cayley graphs as in the title. These are the first explicit examples in infinitely many dimensions n > 2. Moreover, they happen to be expanders.  1. Introduction The girth of a graph is the edge-length of its shortest non-trivial cycle (assigned to be infinity for an acyclic graph). The diameter of a graph is the greatest edge-length distance between any pair of its vertices. We regard a graph Γ as a sequence of its connected components Γ = (Γk )k∈N each of which is endowed with the edge-length distance. Definition 1.1 (dg-bounded graph). [AT18] A graph Γ = (Γk )k∈N is dg-bounded if there exists a constant D > 0 such that for all k ∈ N: diam Γk 6 D. girth Γk In this paper, we focus on dg-bounded graphs with girth Γk → ∞ as k → ∞. A dg-bounded graph with uniformly bounded degree r satisfies girth Γk > D1 diam Γk and diam Γk > logr (|Γk |−1), for r > 3, while diam Γk > 12 (|Γk | − 1) for r = 2. Hence, for such a graph, girth Γk → ∞, whenever |Γk | → ∞ as k → ∞. The existence of r-regular dg-bounded graphs Γ = (Γk )k∈N with girth Γk → ∞ as k → ∞ is a classical result. For instance, first, one can show the existence of large girth graphs using the probabilistic argument of Erdős-Sachs [ES63] (see also [Sac63] for a recursive, on the degree r, construction) or by taking iterated mod 2-covers of a given graph (each iteration at least doubles the girth) and then, following Biggs [Big98, Lemma 3.1], prove that the diameter of the graph with the minimal number of vertices among all graphs of a given degree r > 2 and of girth at least g > 3 is at most g (giving D = 1 for such a graph, called a ‘cage’). However, this does not provide concrete examples and the resulting graphs are not necessarily Cayley graphs. The first explicit examples are given by suitable Cayley graphs of SL2 (Fp ), where Fp = Z/pZ are the integers mod p for a prime p. Namely, the famous explicit construction of large girth graphs by Margulis [Mar82] is made of the Cayley graphs Γp = Cay(SL2(Fp ), {Ap , Bp }) with matrices Ap , Bp being images of Sanov’s generators of the free group (see next section for more details). Besides being a purely combinatorial challenge in a higher dimension in contrast to known results in dimension 2, with potential applications in computer science, our motivation to find explicitly such graphs comes from several recent results in geometric group theory and in metric geometry. For instance, uniformly bounded degree large girth dg-bounded graphs are required in the constructions of infinite finitely generated groups with prescribed subgraphs in their Cayley graphs, so-called ‘infinite monster groups’. A foundational example is Gromov’s random groups that contain infinite expander families in their Cayley graphs [Gro03, AD08]. Gromov’s monster groups do not coarsely embed into a Hilbert space and are counterexamples to an important conjecture in topology, the Baum-Connes conjecture with coefficients [HLS02]. For a more recent result on monster groups see [AT18] (where it is essential to have the large girth dg-bounded graph made of Cayley graphs) and references therein. In all these constructions the imposed combinatorial properties of graphs ensure the existence of appropriate small cancellation labelings of their edges. This in turn guarantees a suitable embedding of the given large girth dg-bounded graph into the Cayley graph of the group (whose generators are labeling letters and relators are defined by the labels of closed cycles). On the other hand, some results in metric geometry use directly large girth and dg-boundedness (no additional labeling is required). For example, in [Ost12], the author constructs first examples of regular large girth graphs with uniformly bounded ℓ1 distortion. Namely, given a uniformly bounded degree large girth dg-bounded graph, he applies the results of [AGv12] to the mod 2-cover of such a graph to conclude a uniform bound on the ℓ1 distortion. Graphs with uniformly bounded ℓ1 distortion are useful, for instance, in the theory of approximation algorithms. Our proof of Main Theorem consists of three steps: for each of the choices of dimensions, n = 2, 3 and n > 4 with n = q t + 1, we prove that our matrices A, B ∈ SLn (Z) satisfy the following: (i) Al and B l generate a free subgroup in SLn (Z) for l > 1 as in the Main Theorem (l = 1 for n = 2), (ii) their reduction mod p generate SLn (Fp ) for all sufficiently large primes p, (iii) the corresponding Cayley graphs of SLn (Fp ) have girth at least cn log p for some cn > 0. Each of these assertions is new for our pair of matrices in case n > 3 and it seems no explicit matrices have been known for each of the properties (i)–(iii) for n > 3. In dimension n = 2, we moreover have property (ii) for all primes p such that a, b 6≡ 0( mod p). For all sufficiently large primes p, (ii) can be obtained from (i) by the Matthews-VasersteinWeisfeiler theorem [MVW84] as the free subgroup of SL2 (Fp ) is non-elementary and hence Zariski dense. Also, (i) implies that the sequence Cay(SL2 (Fp ), {Ap , Bp }) as p → ∞ is an expander [BG08].  4  GOULNARA ARZHANTSEVA AND ARINDAM BISWAS  We do not use any of these deep results in our proof of (i)–(iii) for n = 2 but present direct arguments for the sake of completeness. In dimension n > 3, in contrast to the two-dimensional case, assertion (i) does not yield (ii) since in higher dimensions freeness does not imply Zariski dense, so [MVW84] does not apply immediately. Also, it seems that possible expansion properties of our sequence of Cayley graphs have not been known. Observe that our generators Alp , Bpl of SLn (Fp ) are not images of generators of1 SLn (Z). However, our assertion (ii), combined with [Lub99], does imply that the free subgroup hAl , B l i is Zariski dense. Hence, using [BV12], we obtain that Cay(SLn (Fp ), {Alp , Bpl }) as p → ∞ is indeed an expander. It appears to be the first explicit expander in dimension n > 3 which is large girth dg-bounded, by Main Theorem. Our proof proceeds as follows. For each of the choices of dimensions, we first show properties (i) and (ii), and then use them and known results about growth of small subsets in SLn (Fp ) to obtain (with no use of the expansion properties of the involved graphs as our focus is large girth dg-bounded graphs and, in general, such graphs need not to be expanders) a logarithmic bound on the diameter of the Cayley graphs. This together with (iii) gives the required large girth with bounded diameter-by-girth ratio. Although, our strategy is similar in all dimensions, dimension n = 3 appears to be exceptional. Note also a smaller value of l we can allow in this case in Main Theorem.
