THE TYPE DEFECT OF A SIMPLICIAL COMPLEX  arXiv:1704.01243v2 [math.AC] 18 Apr 2017  HAILONG DAO AND JAY SCHWEIG  Abstract. Fix a field k. When ∆ is a simplicial complex on n vertices with Stanley-Reisner ideal I∆ , we define and study an invariant called the type defect of ∆. Except when ∆ is a single simplex, the type defect of ∆, td(∆), is the difference dimk TorSc (S/I∆ , k) − c, where c is the codimension of ∆ and S = k[x1 , . . . xn ]. We show that this invariant admits surprisingly nice properties. For example, it is wellbehaved when one glues two complexes together along a face. Furthermore, ∆ is Cohen-Macaulay if td(∆) ≤ 0. On the other hand, if ∆ is a simple graph (viewed as a one-dimensional complex), then td(∆0 ) ≥ 0 for every induced subgraph ∆0 of ∆ if and only if ∆ is chordal. Requiring connected induced subgraphs to have type defect zero allows us to define a class of graphs that we call treeish, and which we generalize to simplicial complexes. We then extend some of our chordality results to higher dimensions, proving sharp lower bounds for most Betti numbers of ideals with linear resolution, and classifying when equalities occur. As an application, we prove sharp lower bounds for Betti numbers of graded ideals (not necessarily monomial) with linear resolution.  1. Introduction Let k be a field, let S = k[x1 , x2 , . . . , xn ], and let I be a homogenous ideal such that S/I is Cohen-Macaulay. The type of the quotient S/I is the total Betti number bc (S/I) := dimk TorSc (S/I∆ , k), where c is the codimension of I. That is, the type of S/I is the rank of the free module at position c in a minimal free resolution of S/I over S. In the local setting, this invariant is rather classical. In this paper we study some variants of this concept for (not necessarily) Cohen-Macaulay squarefree monomial ideals. Roughly speaking, we shall show that these invariants behave very well with respect to the “gluing” of simplicial complexes, and that it is strongly linked to important and much-studied properties of complexes such as chordality, linear resolution, or Cohen-Macaulayness. Let ∆ be a (d − 1) dimensional simplicial complex with n vertices. Throughout, let c = n − d, the codimension of ∆. Our key definitions are as follows. Definition 1.1. We define the modified type of ∆ to be: mtype(∆) = dimk TorSc (S/I∆ , k) − dimk TorSc (S/I∆ , k)c where I∆ is the Stanley-Reisner ideal of ∆ and Mc denotes the degree c component of a graded module M . We also define the type defect of ∆, td(∆), to be the difference 1  between its modified type and codimension: td(∆) = mtype(∆) − c. Except in the case when ∆ has one facet, mtype(∆) equals bc (S/I), see 2.2. However, this small modification (see Section 2) actually makes the results and proofs much more elegant. Section 2 deals with preparatory materials. Here we explain the connection between type and Cohen-Macaulayness, Theorem 2.6, which is really just a reformulation of a famous result in commutative algebra. We also prove the gluing results, 2.8 and 2.9, and discuss the modified type of 2-CohenMacaulay complexes, and how the type defect behaves for join of two complexes. It turns out that chordality is equivalent to a requirement on the type defect, as shown by the next theorem. Theorem 3.3. G is chordal iff td(G|W ) ≥ 0 for any W ⊆ V . First assume that G is a chordal graph, let v be a simplicial vertex of G, and let N (v) be its neighbors. Then td(G \ v) ≥ 0, by induction, the base case being immediate. Adding v to G \ v increases the codimension by 1, so we need to show that the type increases by at least 1. If v has only one neighbor w, then the number of components of G\w is one greater than the number of components of (G \ v) \ w. Thus mtype(G) = mtype(G \ v) + 1. Recall that a pure simplicial complex is called strongly facet-connected if, for any two facets F and F 0 , there is a chain of facets F = F0 , F1 , F2 , . . . , Ft = F 0 such that Fi intersects Fi+1 in a codimension-one face for each i. Theorem 3.11. Let Σ be a treeish complex, and let ∆ be an induced subcomplex of Σ that is strongly facet-connected. Then ∆ is a treeish complex. Proof. We think of building Σ via the steps in Definition 3.10. So, suppose Σ has been constructed via subcomplexes as Σ = Σ1 ∪ Σ2 ∪ · · · ∪ Σt , where each Σi is either a full simplex or a simplex boundary, identified along a facet or a codimension-1 face. We prove by induction on t. Let W be the set of vertices of ∆. If t = 1, then ∆ = Σ|W is either a simplex or a boundary of a simplex. Suppose t > 1. Let Σ0 = Σ1 ∪ Σ2 ∪ · · · ∪ Σt−1 and ∆0 = Σ0 |W . By induction ∆0 is treeish. By construction, Σ is obtained from Σ0 by adding one or two vertices and taking a cone of the vertex (or boundary of the cone, in the case of two points) over some face F in Σ0 .
