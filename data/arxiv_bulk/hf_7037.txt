DO MOST POLYNOMIALS GENERATE A PRIME IDEAL?  arXiv:1509.02050v3 [math.AC] 11 Jan 2016  JOSEPHINE YU Abstract. For which monomial supports do most polynomials generate a prime ideal? We give necessary and sufficient conditions for the radical of the ideal to be prime over an algebraically closed field. In characteristic zero, the same conditions give primeness. As an application we show that under the same combinatorial conditions on Newton polytopes, the stable intersection of tropical hypersurfaces is connected through codimension one.  1. Introduction Let us fix monomial supports for some polynomials and ask: Do the polynomials generate a proper prime ideal in a polynomial ring for most choices of coefficients, over an algebraically closed field? The answer is not always yes. Here are some simple examples to illustrate what can go wrong. (1) The polynomials may contain a non-trivial monomial factor; e.g. every polynomial of the form ax + bx2 + cxy ∈ C[x, y] is reducible. Thus it is natural for us to work in the ring of Laurent polynomials instead of the usual polynomial ring. (2) There may be more polynomials than variables; e.g. for most choices of coefficients the polynomials ax + by + c, dx + ey + f, gx + hy + i ∈ C[x±1 , y ±1 ] generate the unit ideal. (2’) We may be in case (2) after a monomial change of coordinates; e.g. for most choices of coefficients the polynomials axz + byw + c, dxz + eyw + f, gxz + hyw + i ∈ C[x±1 , y ±1 , z ±1 , w±1 ] generate the unit ideal, although the number of polynomials is less than the number of variables. (3) The polynomials may form a zero-dimensional system of degree > 1; e.g. for most choices of coefficients the polynomials ax + by + c, dx + ey + f xy ∈ C[x±1 , y ±1 ] generate a zero-dimensional ideal of degree 2, which is not prime. (3’) We may be in case (3) after a monomial change of coordinates; e.g. for most choices of coefficients, the ideal generated by axz + by + c, dxz + ey + f xyz ∈ C[x±1 , y ±1 , z ±1 ] is not prime, although it is not zero-dimensional. (4) A subset of the polynomials may be in case (2’) or (3’); e.g. for most choices of coefficients, the ideal generated by axz + by + c, dxz + ey + f xyz, x + y + z + w ∈ C[x±1 , y ±1 , z ±1 , w±1 ] is not prime. Keywords: sparse polynomials, prime ideals, Newton polytopes, tropical geometry. 1  2  JOSEPHINE YU  Our main result, Theorem 3, gives a precise combinatorial characterization of these obstructions and shows that these are in fact the only ones for irreducibility, and even primeness in characteristic zero. Sufficient conditions for primeness in characteristic p > 0 are less tidy and we do not attempt to list them all here. Here are some obstructions: (5) Monomials in one of the polynomials may all be p-th powers; e.g. the ideal generated by axp + by p z 2p + c is not prime over characteristic p > 0 for general coefficients a, b, c. (5’) We may be in the previous case after some cancellation; e.g. the ideal generated by axp + by p + cz and dy p + ewp + f z is not prime over characteristic p > 0 for general coefficients a, b, c, d, e, f . We state and prove the main result in the next section and discuss an application to tropical geometry in Section 3. 2. Main Theorem ±1 Let k be an algebraically closed field and R = k[x±1 1 , . . . , xn ] be the ring of Laurent polynomials in n variables over k. Let k ≤ n, and fix A1 , . . . , Ak ⊂ Zn . Consider the space (k∗ )A1 × · · · × (k∗ )Ak of polynomials (f1 , . . . , fk ) whose supports are A1 , . . . , Ak respectively. We say that a property holds for general elements in (k∗ )A1 × · · · × (k∗ )An if it holds on a Zariski open dense subset. We work in the Laurent polynomial ring to disregard monomial factors from the polynomials. By translating each Aj by any of its members, and equivalently factoring out a Laurent monomial from fj , if necessary, we will assume for the rest of the paper that 0 ∈ Aj for all j ∈ [k] := {1, . . . , k}. Let us first ask: When do general polynomials generate a proper ideal?  Lemma 1. Suppose 1 ≤ k ≤ n, A1 , . . . , Ak ⊂ Zn and 0 ∈ Aj for all j. General polynomials f1 , . . . , fk with supports A1 , . . . , Ak respectively generate a proper ideal in the Laurent polynomial ring R if and only if one, hence both, of the following equivalent conditions hold. S (1) For every non-empty subset J ⊆ [k], dim spanQ j∈J Aj ≥ |J|. (2) There exist u1 ∈ A1 , . . . , uk ∈ Ak that are linearly independent. Proof. Since k is algebraically closed, by Hilbert’s Nullstellensatz, the ideal hf1 , . . . , fk i in R is proper if and only if the system of equations f1 = · · · = fk = 0 has a solution in (k∗ )n . This happens for general f1 , . . . , fk if and only if the sparse toric resultant of (A1 , . . . , Ak ) is identically zero, or equivalently the resultant variety has codimension zero. The conditions (1) and (2) follow from the codimension formulas from [Stu94, Theorem 1.1] and [JY13, Theorem 2.23] respectively. The equivalence of (1) and (2) also follows directly from Perfect’s generalization of Hall’s marriage theorem [Per69], which has an easy combinatorial proof, so in fact we only need one of [Stu94, JY13].  We say that the collection (A1 , . . . , Ak ) contains an independent transversal if it satisfies (1) or (2) above. When k = n, this is precisely the condition for the mixed volume of conv(A1 ), . . . , conv(Ak ) to be non-zero.  DO MOST POLYNOMIALS GENERATE A PRIME IDEAL?  3  Let us now turn to the question of primeness. We first introduce a combinatorial condition stronger than having an independent transversal. Lemma/Definition 2. The following are equivalent for any A1 , . . . , Ak ⊂ Zn . S (1) For every non-empty subset J ⊆ [k], dim spanQ j∈J Aj ≥ |J| + 1. (2) For every 1 ≤ j ≤ k, there exist v1 ∈ A1 , . . . , vj−1 ∈ Aj−1 , and u1 , u2 ∈ Aj such that v1 , . . . , vj−1 , u1 , u2 are linearly independent. We will call this the dragon marriage independent transversal condition, or DMIT for short. When the disjoint union of A1 , . . . , Ak is linearly independent, we obtain the dragon marriage condition of Postnikov [Pos09]. Proof. For every non-empty J ⊆ [k], by taking j to be the largest element in J, we get (2) ⇒ (1). For the converse, let u be any non-zero element in Aj and let φ be a linear projection of Qn onto an n − 1 P dimensional subspace such that φ(u) = 0. By the condition (1), we have dim spanQ j∈J φ(Aj ) ≥ |J| for all non-empty J ⊆ [k]. By equivalence of (1) and (2) in Lemma 1, there exist v1 ∈ A1 , . . . , vj ∈ Aj such that φ(v1 ), . . . , φ(vj ) are linearly independent. It follows that v1 , . . . , vj , u are linearly independent.  We can now prove the main theorem: Theorem 3. Suppose 1 ≤ k ≤ n, A1 , . . . , Ak ⊂ Zn and 0 ∈ Aj for all j. Stable intersection of tropical hypersurfaces I want to point out, to readers familiar with tropical geometry, a consequence of the main theorem on connectivity of stable intersection of tropical hypersurfaces. Please refer to [MS15, JY15] for background on tropical hypersurfaces and their stable intersections. Corollary 5. Suppose the collection (A1 , . . . , Ak ) satisfies the condition in Theorem 3. Then for any tropical polynomials with corresponding supports, the stable intersection of their tropical hypersurfaces is connected through codimension one. Proof. The stable intersection of tropical hypersurfaces is the tropicalization of an ideal generated by some general polynomials with the given Newton polytope [OP13, JY15], over any algebraically closed field. By Theorem 3 above, the zero set of this ideal is irreducible. The result then follows from [CP12].  This result has applications in computational tropical geometry, where we need connectivity for graph traversal algorithms, such as those in [Jen, Mal14], to work correctly. In the constant-coefficient case, when the tropical hypersurfaces are fans, the stable intersection is connected through codimension one without any assumption on the supports [JY13, JY15]. 4. Open Problems What are precise combinatorial conditions for primeness in positive characteristic? See Example (5’) in the Introduction. Although Corollary 5 is a purely combinatorial statement about mixed subdivisions of polytopes and their dual complexes, I do not know of a combinatorial proof, even for the case of two tropical hypersurfaces in R3 . It will be interesting to find a direct proof and also to study combinatorial and topological properties of the stable intersection, such as shellability. The DMIT condition is checkable in polynomial time in the size of the input (A1 , . . . , Ak ), as follows. By the proof of Lemma 2, the DMIT condition holds if and only if for every j ∈ [k] and for every u ∈ Aj , (φu (A1 ), . . . , φu (Aj )) contains an independent transversal, where φu is a linear projection from Qn to an n − 1 dimensional subspace such that φu (u) = 0. The existence of independent transversal can be checked in polynomial time using matroid intersection as in [JY13, Theorem 2.29]. I do not know whether the condition of Theorem 3, which is weaker than DMIT, is checkable in polynomial time. In particular, can we efficiently check whether the mixed volume is equal to 1? We can study the (Zariski closure of) the locus of coefficients for which smoothness, reducedness, or irreducibility fails. For the case when the number of polynomials is  6  JOSEPHINE YU  equal to the number of variables, the non-smooth locus is the mixed discriminant studied in [CCD+ 13], which conincides with the A-discriminant of the Cayley sum of A1 , . . . , Ak .
