ON QUASI-ISOMETRIC NILPOTENT LIE GROUPS  arXiv:1710.04542v2 [math.GR] 20 Nov 2017  MANUEL AMANN  Abstract. In this article we provide evidence for a well-known conjecture which states that quasi-isometric simply-connected nilpotent Lie groups are isomorphic. We do so by constructing new examples which are rigid in the sense that whenever they are quasi-isometric to any other simply-connected nilpotent Lie group, the groups are actually isomorphic.  Introduction This article centers around the following Conjecture 0.1. Quasi-isometric simply-connected nilpotent Lie groups are isomorphic. We slightly rephrase the conjecture. We call a simply-connected nilpotent Lie group G rigid if any simply-connected nilpotent Lie group G0 quasiisometric to G is actually isomorphic to G. There was a proof of Conjecture 0.1 announced in the first version and withdrawn in the second version of the article [4]. It is the goal of this article to construct new examples of rigid nilpotent Lie groups. By a result of Pansu it is known that the associated Carnot graded nilpotent Lie algebras are isomorphic, by a result of Sauer quasi-isometric nilpotent Lie groups yield Lie algebras with isomorphic real cohomology algebras. Hence, in order to determine rigidity of a simply-connected nilpotent Lie group, it suffices to show that whenever there is a second group with isomorphic associated Carnot graded Lie algebra and isomorphic real cohomology algebra, then both Lie algebras are actually already isomorphic. We shall do so by passing to a dual description in real homotopy theory. We provide the following new examples—drawing on the 1:1-correspondence between simply-connected Lie groups and Lie algebras. In particular, the second and fourth examples are not Carnot graded. Theorem A. Let k ≥ 1 for (1) and (3), suppose that k ∈ {2, 3} for (2). The method of this proof is to first transcribe the situation to the realm of minimal Sullivan models. We then combine the results of Sauer and Pansu to carefully analyse our examples paralleled by an investigation of the minimal Sullivan model of a potentially quasi-isometric Lie group. The realisation as a nilpotent Lie group the Lie algebra of which is rationally equivalent to the Lie algebra we start with is provided by [1, Section 5]. Thus it will suffice to investigate the corresponding Sullivan models in Section 2. Finally, in Section 3 (see Theorem 3.1) we provide a probably well-known example of two nilpotent Lie algebras which have isomorphic associated Carnot graded Lie algebras and isomorphic cohomology, but which are  ON QUASI-ISOMETRIC NILPOTENT LIE GROUPS  3  not isomorphic themselves. This shows that a general confirmation of the conjecture just from the results by Pansu and Sauer is a priori not possible. From the point of view of rational homotopy theory we aim to find models of nilpotent Lie algebras which are so to say “intrinsically formal” within the class of nilpotent Lie algebras with the same associated Carnot graded algebra. That is, we want to find nilpotent Lie algebras already determined by their associated Carnot graded algebra and their cohomology type. There is a related and converse question: Yves Félix asked whether a rational cohomology type could be realised by non-isomorphic elliptic rational homotopy types. There are indeed examples of this form (see [5], [6]). We reduced Conjecture 0.1 to the following interesting and somewhat converse Question. How many nilpotent Lie algebras are there sharing one associated Carnot graded type, yet possessing pairwise distinct cohomology rings? Our examples at least yield some more instances upon which the corresponding decision question can be answered in the affirmative. As we were informed by Yves Cornulier for example in dimension 7 there are 4 families sharing a common associated graded algebra. Structure of the article. Prerequisites The results draw on two properties known to be implied by quasi-isometry (see [7],[8]). Theorem 1.1 (Sauer). Quasi-isometric finitely generated nilpotent groups possess Malcev completions with isomorphic real graded cohomology algebras. Recall that by a theorem of Malcev any finitely generated torsion-free nilpotent group Γ is discretely and cocompactly embedded in a unique simplyconnected nilpotent Lie group G, the so-called (real) Malcev completion of Γ. Thus Γ has an associated real Lie algebra g and the real cohomology algebras of g and Γ are isomorphic. For simply-connected nilpotent Lie groups the real Malcev completion agrees with the group itself. Theorem 1.2 (Pansu).
