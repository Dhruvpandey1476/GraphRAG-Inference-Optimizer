Retrodirective Multi-User Wireless Power Transfer with Massive MIMO  arXiv:1706.07549v1 [cs.IT] 23 Jun 2017  Seunghyun Lee, Student Member, IEEE, Yong Zeng, Member, IEEE, and Rui Zhang, Fellow, IEEE Abstract—This letter studies a radio-frequency (RF) multiuser wireless power transfer (WPT) system, where an energy transmitter (ET) with a large number of antennas delivers energy wirelessly to multiple distributed energy receivers (ERs). We investigate a low-complexity WPT scheme based on the retrodirective beamforming technique, where all ERs send a common beacon signal simultaneously to the ET in the uplink and the ET simply conjugates and amplifies its received sum-signal and transmits to all ERs in the downlink for WPT. We show that such a low-complexity scheme achieves the massive multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) energy beamforming gain. However, a “doubly near-far” issue exists due to the round-trip (uplink beacon and downlink WPT) signal propagation loss where the harvested power of a far ER from the ET can be significantly lower than that of a near ER if the same uplink beacon power is used. To tackle this problem, we propose a distributed uplink beacon power update algorithm, where each ER independently adjusts the beacon power based on its current harvested power in an iterative manner. It is shown that the proposed algorithm converges quickly to a unique fixed-point solution, which helps achieve the desired user fairness with best efforts. Index Terms—Wireless power transfer, retrodirective transmission, energy beamforming, massive MIMO, distributed power control.  I. I NTRODUCTION Radio-frequency (RF) transmission enabled wireless power transfer (WPT) offers a cost-effective solution for supplying power perceptually to wireless devices in energy-constrained networks [1], [2]. Compared to alternative WPT technologies such as inductive coupling, RF enabled WPT has several promising advantages such as wide coverage, low production cost, and smaller transmitter and receiver form factors, etc. In particular, to overcome the significant power loss over distance in RF WPT, the technique of multi-antenna enabled directional transmission or energy beamforming (EB) is essential (see, e.g., [2] and the references therein). However, for practical implementation of EB, accurate knowledge of the channel state information (CSI) is required at the energy transmitter (ET). Various CSI acquisition methods designed for WPT have been proposed, including the forward-link training with receiver CSI feedback [3], the reverse-link training by exploiting channel reciprocity [4], [5], and the energy-feedback based training [6]–[8]. However, the training and feedback overhead of the forward-link training and energy-feedback based training methods can be practically high, especially in multi-user WPT systems with a large number of energy receivers (ERs) and/or massive multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) WPT systems with a large number of transmit antennas. The reverse-link training approach successfully overcomes this issue as there is no ER feedback required and the training length is independent The authors are with the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering, National University of Singapore, Singapore (email: {s.lee, elezeng, elezhang}@nus.edu.sg).  of the number of transmit antennas, which is an appealing advantage for massive MIMO WPT [9], [10]. Nevertheless, the performance of this method critically depends on multi-user orthogonal pilots assignment among the ERs, which causes the so-called “pilot contamination” problem in massive MIMO WPT systems with many ETs and ERs [11]. Furthermore, multi-user WPT systems in general suffer from the energy near-far problem [1], where the harvested power of ERs can vary significantly depending on their distances from the ET. Thus, how to achieve a balanced performance among nearfar ERs and yet with affordable low complexity is still challenging in implementing multi-user WPT systems in practice, especially with massive MIMO. A promising low-complexity WPT scheme to achieve the above goal is the retrodirective beamforming technique [2]. In this technique, the ERs transmit a common beacon signal simultaneously to the ET in the uplink, and the ET simply amplifies the conjugate of the received sum-signal at each of its antennas and broadcasts to all ERs in the downlink for WPT. In this letter, we study a multi-user massive MIMO WPT system based on the technique of retrodirective EB. An important practical issue in retrodirective-based multi-user WPT is the so-called “doubly near-far” problem [2], where the uplink beacon signal sent by a far ER is weakly received at the ET as compared to that of a near ER. Thus, the retrodirective EB becomes ineffective for the far ER in the downlink WPT, resulting in unfair performance among nearfar ERs. To resolve this problem, we propose a distributed beacon power update algorithm, where each ER independently adjusts the beacon power based on its current harvested power in an iterative manner, subject to the maximum beacon power constraint. It is shown that with the proposed algorithm, the uplink beacon powers converge quickly to a unique fixed-point solution. Moreover, the converged beacon powers of the near ERs are effectively reduced as compared to those of the far ERs, thus helping to achieve the desired performance balance among all ERs with best efforts. Last, if the same beacon power is used by all ERs, i.e., p1 = ... = pK , (6) will result in unfair performance among near-far  3  ERs, since the dominant harvested power of each ER (see the second term in (6)) is proportional to βk2 . This reveals a critical “doubly near-far” problem in retrodirective-based multi-user WPT; while in order to achieve the balanced harvested power for all ERs, it follows from (6) that ERs with smaller βk2 (i.e., more far-away from the ET) need to use significantly higher beacon power than those with larger βk2 (i.e., nearer to the ER), due to the round-trip power loss in both uplink beacon transmission and downlink WPT. It is worth noting that an effective method to mitigate the above doubly near-far problem is that the ER first obtains the knowledge of all βk ’s, k = 1, ..., K, via e.g., the reverse-link training [4], [5] and then assigns a proper value of pk to each ERk based on (6) to balance the harvested power among nearfar ERs. However, this method requires multi-user orthogonal pilots assignment among ERs for uplink training as well as the downlink transmission of the optimized beacon powers to individual ERs, which is costly especially when the number of ETs/ERs is large. Thus, in the following section, we propose a distributed algorithm for each ER to independently update its beacon power in an iterative manner based only on its own received power and harvested power target, thus helping to achieve the desired user fairness for the low-complexity retrodirective-based WPT. IV. D ISTRIBUTED B EACON -P OWER U PDATE Let Q̄k ≥ 0, k = 1, ..., K, denote the harvested power target that ERk aims to achieve, i.e., Qk (p) ≥ Q̄k , where Qk (p) is given in (6). Without loss of generality, we assume that Q̄k ≥ Pt βk , k = 1, ..., K, since each ERk is guaranteed to harvest at least Pt βk amount of power even without transmitting a beacon signal, i.e., pk = 0, as discussed in Section III. For convenience, we first define qk (p) = Qk (p) − Pt βk and q̄k = Q̄k − Pt βk , k = 1, ..., K. It then follows that Qk (p) ≥ Q̄k is equivalent to qk (p) ≥ q̄k , k = 1, ..., K. Note that for the above proposed scheme, each ERk only needs to know the value of Pt βk , k = 1, ..., K. This can be easily obtained in an initial block (n = 0) by each ERk via setting pk [0] = 0 and then measuring the harvested power. The power-update rule given in (7) implies that each ERk will increase its beacon power if qk (p[n]) < q̄k , provided that 0 < pk [n] < Pmax , or decrease it if qk (p[n]) > q̄k and pk [n] > 0.
