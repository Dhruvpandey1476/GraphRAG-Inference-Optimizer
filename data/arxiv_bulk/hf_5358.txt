However, many problems need concentration inequalities to deal with Markov-dependent random variables. For example, linear regression, lasso regression and sparse covariance matrix estimation methods have been applied to time series data with a Markovian nature, see e.g. Yuan and Kendziorski (2006); Nardi and Rinaldo (2011); Cai et al. (2010); Fan et al. (2016), but the existing analyses of these methods usually assume independence among data samples. Concentration inequalities for Markov-dependent data samples are in need to fill this gap. As another example, a Markov chain Monte Carlo method (Gilks, 2005) approximates a complicated integral by averaging samples from a well-behaved Markov chain. A sharp and clear concentration inequality for this average determines how long the Markov chain should run to control the approximation error. A third example is the multi-armed bandit problem with Markovian rewards. Researchers have recognized three fundamental formalizations of multi-armed bandit problems depending on the nature of the reward process: independently and identically distributed (i.i.d.), adversarial, and Markovian (Bubeck and Cesa-Bianchi, 2012). The Markovian formalization has been much less studied than the other two, primarily because bandit algorithms rely on the concentration of reward draws to identify the optimal arm, but unfortunately powerful concentration inequalities for Markovian rewards are sparse. There are mainly two approaches in the literature. The first approach uses the operator theory in Hilbert spaces and obtains inequalities involving the L2 (π)-spectral gap. For a finite-state-space, reversible Markov chain and a time-independent bounded function, Gillman (1993) provided the first Hoeffding-type inequality, which was later improved by Dinwoodie (1995). In the same setting, León and Perron (2004) proved a sharp Hoeffding-type inequality by considering a two-state Markov chain with a convexly majorized transition kernel. Miasojedow (2014) extended León and Perron (2004)’s results to general-state-space Markov chains, but his inequality has a looser multiplicative coefficient. This paper provides both Hoeffding’s lemma and Hoeffding’s inequality for general-state-space Markov chains and time-dependent functions, which is more powerful than the Hoeffding-type inequalities of León and Perron (2004) and Miasojedow (2014). A detailed comparison of our results and theirs is given at the end of this subsection. The first approach has also produced Bernstein-type inequalities for a finite-state-space Markov chain and a time-independent bounded function with known variance. For example, see Theorem 1.1 in Lezaud (1998) and Theorems 3.3, 3.4 in Paulin (2015). These Bernsteintype inequalities reduce to Hoeffding-type inequalities if substituting the variance of the bounded function f : x 7→ [a, b] with its natural upper bound (b − a)2 /4. But the resulting Hoeffding-type inequalities are not as sharp as those directly established. Moreover, to the best of our knowledge, there is no Bernstein-type inequality for general-state-space Markov chains. The second approach in the literature exploits the minorization or drift conditions, see e.g. Glynn and Ormoneit (2002); Douc, Moulines, Olsson and van Handel (2011); Adamczak and Bednorz (2015). Their inequalities apply for Markov chains which may not admit L2 (π)-spectral gaps, but have less explicit constants and more complicated expressions. In this paper, we improve on Hoeffding-type inequalities of León and Perron (2004) and Miasojedow (2014) in three folds. First, our main result in Theorem 2.1 considers timedependent functions fi with different ranges [ai , bi ], while their Hoeffding-type inequalities consider time-independent functions only. Second, in the setting of time-independent functions f1 = · · · = fn = f , elements newly explored in this paper directly strengthen their Hoeffding-type inequalities. Theorem 2.2 extends León and Perron (2004)’s to general-statespace, non-reversible Markov chains and covers theirs as a special case for finite-state-space, reversible Markov chains. This theorem also sharpens the multiplicative coefficient in Miasojedow (2014)’s. Lastly but most importantly, we provide an explicit bound for the P moment generating function (mgf) of ni=1 fi (Xi ), which is missing in all previous works. This sub-Gaussian property, we believe, is the essence of Hoeffding’s inequality and explicitly characterizes the behaviors of Markov-dependent random variables. This quantity has played a central role in the literature studying Markov chains by spectral methods, e.g. the central limit theorem for Metropolis-Hastings algorithm (Geyer, 1992), concentration inequalities of Markov-dependent random variables (Gillman, 1993; Dinwoodie, 1995; Lezaud, 1998; León and Perron, 2004; Miasojedow, 2014; Paulin, 2015), the mean squared error of the Markov chain Monte Carlo estimators (Rudolf, 2012), and approximate transition kernels in Markov chain Monte Carlo algorithms (Negrea and Rosenthal, 2017). For a reversible Markov chain, a positive L2 (π)-spectral gap exists if and only if the Markov chain is geometrically ergodic, see e.g. (Roberts and Rosenthal, 1997, Theorem 2.1) and (Conway, 2013, Proposition VIII.1.11). For non-reversible Markov chains, the L2 (π)spectral gap assumption is stronger than geometric ergodicity (Kontoyiannis and Meyn, 2012, Theorem 1.4). Some works such as (Kontoyiannis and Meyn, 2012) define the L2 (π)spectral gap in a slightly different way, which does not coincide with ours for non-reversible Markov chains. If using their definition and assuming the L2 (π)-spectral gap exists, we have a modified version of Theorem 2.1 in Subsection A.4.  3  Preliminaries  Throughout the paper, we assume the state space X equipped with a sigma-algebra B is nice 2 .
