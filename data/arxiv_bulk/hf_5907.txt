Random-Walks on Graphs and Approximation of l2-Invariants  arXiv:1607.08013v1 [math.GR] 27 Jul 2016  Zenas Wong, Andrew Kricker Abstract Right multiplication operators Rw : l2 G → l2 G, w ∈ C[G], are interpreted as random-walk operators on labelled graphs that are analogous to Cayley graphs. Applying a generalization of the graph convergence defined by R. Grigorchuk and A. Żuk [5] gives a new proof and interpretation of a special case of W. Lück’s famous Theorem on the Approximation of l2 -Betti numbers for countable residually finite groups. In particular, using this interpretation, the proof follows quickly from standard theorems about the weak convergence of probability measures that are characterized by their moments.  1  Introduction  A key area of interest in the theory of l2 -invariants is the study of the spectral density function F (Rw ) : [0, ∞) → [0, ∞) of a G-equivariant bounded operator Rw : l2 G → l2 G, given by right multiplication of w (an element of the complex group ring C[G]) on the Hilbert space l2 G of square summable formal sums: X X l2 G = { λg g : λg ∈ C, |λg |2 < ∞}. g∈G  g∈G  F (Rw ) is used to define l2 -invariants of Rw , such as the l2 -Betti number, Novikov-Shubin invariant, and Fuglede-Kadison determinant. In general, F (Rw ) is difficult to compute, and there are few instances of actual computations of the l2 -invariants of right multiplication operators. For example, for any w ∈ C[G] with G = Zd , recent work by W. Lück [4] provided bounds on the spectral density function of Rw , and established that the NovikovShubin invariant and Fuglede-Kadison determinant of Rw are both positive. In [10], L. Grabowski discovered particular groups G for which certain elements w ∈ C[G] are such that the l2 -Betti number of Rw is irrational. This paper studies the spectral density function of Rw using the theory of random walk (or Markov) operators of groups. This relies on [5], where R. Grigorchuk and A. Żuk defined convergence of sequences of locally finite marked graphs, and showed that the spectral measure associated to the random-walk (or Markov) operator of such a graph is continuous with respect to this type of graph convergence. This was subsequently used in [6] to explicitly compute the spectrum of a Markov operator of the lamplighter group, and the approach 1  was further extended in [8] to Markov operators of groups generated by Cayley automata. An important connection between these 2 theories is that Markov operators of a finitely generated group G set up the following Correspondence (⋆) between: 1. Random walks on the Cayley graph ΓG,S of G with respect to a finite symmetric generating set S, and 2. G-equivariant bounded operators Rm : l2 GP→ l2 G, given by right multipli1 cation of the self-adjoint element m = |S| s∈S s ∈ C[G]. Rm is known as the random-walk operator (or Markov operator) associated to the Cayley graph ΓG,S . In particular, computing the spectral measures of random walk operators on the Cayley graph ΓG,S gives information on the spectral density function of Rm and the associated l2 -invariants. For example, the main result of [6] was used to give a counter-example [9] to the Strong Atiyah Conjecture concerning the range of values of l2 -Betti numbers. The aim of this paper is to extend this correspondence to self-adjoint elements z = ww∗ ∈ C[G] for an arbitrary element w of C[G], and use it to study F (Rw ). The approach is to introduce a graph Γ that is analogous to Cayley graphs, such that right multiplication by w is the random walk operator associated to Γ. In keeping with Correspondence (⋆), Rw will be called the Markovtype operator associated to Γ. When G is a finitely generated residually finite group, this approach yields a new proof for the pointwise almost-everywhere convergence to F (Rw ) by means of finite-dimensional analogues. To explain this, one will fix the following notations: Setting. Let G be a finitely generated residually finite group. Suppose that G has a nested sequence of finite index normal subgroups: {Kn ⊳ G : [G : Kn ] < ∞, Kn ⊇ Kn+1 }n∈N , such that n∈N Kn = {1}. For A ∈ Ma×b (C[G]), let RA : (l2 G)a → (l2 G)b be the operator given by right multiplication by A. While the techniques behind the new proof of Theorem 1 constitute well-known results, they are nonetheless part of an important and increasingly useful approach of applying tools from geometric group theory to give insights into conjectures involving spectral density functions of arbitrary right multiplication operators and their associated l2 -invariants. Examples of the usefulness of this approach can be seen in [18, 11]. This paper grew out of our attempts to understand the methods to compute l2 -Alexander invariant for knots K, which are such that the fundamental group π1 (S3 \K) (of the complement of K in the 3-sphere S3 ) is a countable residually finite group. This is an l2 -invariant analogue of twisted Alexander polynomials that was introduced by W. Li and W. Zhang in [19]. In particular, knots such as 2-bridge knots K are such that G = π1 (S3 \K) admits a deficiency 1 presentation, and therefore the l2 -Alexander invariant of K can, in principle, be computed from an element w of C[G] (although for most cases, direct computations are difficult to perform). The l2 -Alexander invariant is an example of an l2 -Alexander torsion; l2 -Alexander torsions were studied by J. Dubois, S. Friedl, and W. Lück in [20, 21]. The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 presents relevant theory on l2 invariants of right multiplication operators, and the weak convergence of measures. Section 3 presents the main geometric group theoretic tool of labelled connected marked graphs (denoted lcmg’s) and the corresponding concept of convergence. Section 4 gives the proof of the main result.  2 2.1  Preliminaries l2 -Invariants  For details on the theory of l2 -invariants, see [1], Chapters 1, 2, and 13. Let G be a discrete group. Let {µn } be a sequence of probability measures on a metric space X (with Borel σ-algebra B(X)). One says that {µn } converges weakly to a probability measure µ if limn→∞ µn (B) = µ(B) for all B ∈ B(X) such that µ(∂B) = 0, where ∂B is the boundary of B. The limit measure of weak convergence can be shown to be unique. Now for X = R, let µ : B(R) → R≥0 be a probability measure on R. The cumulative distribution function (CDF) Fµ : R → R≥0 associated to µ is Fµ (x) := µ(−∞, x]. Note that the intervals (−∞, x] have measure zero boundary. Proposition 1 (Theorem 13.23, [14]). For probability measures {µn }n∈N and µ on B(R), the following are equivalent: 1. {µn } converges weakly to µ. 2. For all x such that Fµ is continuous, limn→∞ Fµn (x) = Fµ (x). Let P(R) be the set of Borel probability measures on R such that all polynomials with real coefficients (denoted R[x]) belong to L1 (µ); define an equivalence R R relation on P(R): µ1 ∼ µ2 if and only if for every p ∈ R[x], R p dµ1 = R p dµ2 . For µ ∈ RP(R) and a measurable function f on R, the expected value of f is Eµ [f ] = R f (x)dµ(x). For k ∈ N, the kth moment of µ is µ(k) = Eµ [xk ]. µ is 4  said to be characterized by its moments if the equivalence class of µ consists of a single measure. By the Stone-Weierstrass Theorem, any compactly supported measure is characterized by its moments. The following result is a classical result in the theory of probability measures. This is a slight extension of the convergence of connected marked graphs as described in [5]. The set of all lcmg’s forms a metric space, and will be denoted by LCMG. For each lcmg (X, v), one obtains an operator MX,v that describes a random walk on the lcmg; MX,v will be called the Markov-type operator associated to X. To MX,v will be associated a probability measure µX v , which will be called the Kesten spectral measure of X at v. These spectral measures generalize the Kesten spectral measures that occur in [7], which are defined for Markov operators of Cayley graphs; see also [8]. The key result is that the map (X, v) 7→ MX,v 7→ µX v is weakly continuous with respect to convergence of lcmg’s. A labelled connected marked graph (lcmg) is a connected directed graph X = (V, E) with a distinguished (or marked) vertex v ∈ V (X), such that: 1. V (X) and E(X) are both countable. 2. Each vertex has finite in and out degrees. 3. Edges of X carry labels in C, which are determined by a map LX : E(X) → C. Edges are allowed to have label 0 ∈ C. 4. For each vertex, at most one self-loop is allowed at each vertex. 5. For any 2 vertices v1 , v2 ∈ V , there are no multiple directed edges from v1 to v2 ; the unique directed edge v1 → v2 will be denoted by (v1 , v2 ). By ignoring the C-labels on edges and taking the underlying graph, there is a metric dX on V , where dX (v1 , v2 ) is given by the minimum length of a directed path in X from v1 to v2 , where each edge is assigned length 1.
