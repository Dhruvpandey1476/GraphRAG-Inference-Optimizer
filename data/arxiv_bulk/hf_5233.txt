This paper is devoted to present some characterizations for a local ring to be generically Gorenstein and Gorenstein. We first describe its connection with linkage theory and next we investigate by means of δ -invariant.  1. I NTRODUCTION Characterizations of important classes of commutative noetherian rings are in the main interests of the authors. There are a vast number of papers characterizing rings to be regular, Gorenstein, generically Gorenstein, Cohen-Macaulay, etc (see [19, Theorem 4.3], [20, Theorem 2.3], [9, Theorem 1.3], [18, Theorem 2.3], [8, Theorem 1]). A commutative noetherian R is called generically Gorenstein whenever Rp is Gorenstein for all minimal prime ideal p of R. It is well known that if (R, m) is Cohen-Macaulay local ring with canonical module then R is generically Gorenstein if and only if the canonical module is isomorphic to an ideal of R (see [4, Proposion 3.3.18]). In section 2, we are interested in characterization of a ring to be Gorenstein by means of linkage theory. We next describe how generically Gorenstein-ness of a ring with positive dimension may be reducible to the one with smaller dimension. As a result a class of non-generically Gorenstein ring may be recognized. In section 3, we investigate the delta invariant in order to characterize rings to be generically Gorenstein, Gorenstein, or regular. In section 4, we are interested in characterization of a Gorenstein ring to be non-regular by means of higher delta invariant. Throughout (R, m) is a commutative local noetherian ring with maximal ideal m and residue field k = R/m, and all modules are finite (i.e. finitely generated). 2.  CHARACTERIZATION BY LINKAGE THEORY  The notion of linkage of ideals in commutative algebra is invented by Peskine and Szpiro [16]. Two ideals I and J in a Cohen-Macaulay local ring R are said to be linked if there is a regular sequence a in their intersection such that I = (a) :R J and J = (a) :R I. They have shown that the Cohen-Macaulay-ness property is preserved under linkage over Gorenstein local rings and provided a counterexample to show that the above result is no longer true if the base ring is Cohen-Macaulay but not Gorenstein. We will discuss this situation in section 4. Definition 2.1. Let (R, m) be a local ring, I and J are ideals of R. The ideal I is said to be linked to J if I = (0 :R J) and J = (0 :R I). In this case I is called a linked ideal. We call an ideal I to be generically linked if for any p ∈ Spec R with ht p = 0 and I ⊂ p, the ideal IRp is linked in Rp . Note that a local ring R is generically Gorenstein if and only if any ideal of height 0 is generically linked. 1  Dibaei was supported in part by a grant from ... 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification. 13C14, 13D02, 13H10, 16G50, 16E65, 13H05. This definition of delta is used by Ding [6]. We recall the basic properties of delta invariant. Proposition 3.1. [15, Corollary 11.26] and [3, Lemma 1.2]. (i) δR (M1 ⊕ M2 ) = δR (M1 ) + δR (M2 ) when M = M1 ⊕ M2 (ii) If M −→ N is a surjective homomorphism then δR (M) ≥ δR (N). (iii) δR (M) ≤ µ (M) (iv) δR (k) = 1 if and only if R is regular.
