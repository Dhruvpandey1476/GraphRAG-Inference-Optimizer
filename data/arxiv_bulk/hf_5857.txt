arXiv:1302.1012v1 [math.AC] 5 Feb 2013  The unicity of real Picard–Vessiot fields Teresa Crespo, Zbigniew Hajto and Marius van der Put Abstract Using Deligne’s work on Tannakian categories, the unicity of real Picard-Vessiot ﬁelds for diﬀerential modules over a real diﬀerential ﬁeld is derived. The inverse problem for real forms of a semi-simple group is treated. Some examples illustrate the relations between differential modules, Picard–Vessiot ﬁelds and real forms of a group.  1  Introduction  K denotes a real differential field with field of constants k. We suppose that k 6= K and that k is a real closed field. Let M denote a differential module over K of dimension d, represented by a matrix differential equation y ′ = Ay where A is a d × d-matrix with entries in K. A Picard–Vessiot field L for M/K is a field extension of K such that: (a) L is equipped with a differentiation extending the one of K, (b) M has a full space of solutions over L, i.e., there exists an invertible d × d-matrix F (called a fundamental matrix) with entries in L satisfying F ′ = AF , (c) L is (as a field) generated over K by the entries of F , (d) the field of constants of L is again k. A real Picard–Vessiot field L for M/K is a Picard–Vessiot field which is also a real field. In [CHS1] and [CHS2] the existence of a real Picard–Vessiot field is proved using results of Kolchin. A Picard–Vessiot field L for L has the properties: k is the field of constants of L, the solution space V = {v ∈ L| L(v) = 0} is a k-linear space of dimension d and L is generated over the field K by V and all the derivatives of the elements in V . One writes L = K < V > for this last property. Lemma 2.1 Let L1 , L2 be two real Picard–Vessiot fields for M over K. Suppose that L1 and L2 have total orderings extending a total ordering τ on K. Let K r ⊃ K be the real closure of K inducing the total ordering τ . Then: The fields L1 , L2 induce Picard–Vessiot fields L̃1 , L̃2 for K r ⊗ M over K r . These fields are isomorphic as differential field extensions of K r if and only if L1 and L2 are isomorphic as differential field extensions of K. 3  Proof. Let, for j = 1, 2, τj be a total ordering on Lj inducing τ on K and let Lrj be the real closure of Lj which induces the ordering τj . The algebraic closure Kj of K in Lrj is real closed. Since τj induces τ , there exists a Klinear isomorphism φj : K r → Kj . This isomorphism is unique since the only K-linear automorphism of K r is the identity. We will identify Kj with K r . Let Vj ⊂ Lj denote the solution space of M. Then, for j = 1, 2, the field L̃j := K r < Vj >⊂ Lrj is a real Picard–Vessiot field for K r ⊗ M. Clearly ψ(V1 ) = V2 and ψ induces therefore a K-linear differential isomorphism L1 = K < V1 >→ L2 = K < V2 >. Let L1 , L2 denote two real Picard-Vessiot fields for a differential module M/K. Write ωj = ωLj :< M >⊗ → vect(k) for the corresponding fibre functors. ⊗ Let G = Aut⊗ k (ω1 ). Then Isomk (ω1 , ω2 ) is a G-torsor over k corresponding to an element ξ ∈ H 1 ({1, σ}, G(k(i))), where {1, σ} is Gal(k(i)/k), represented by a 1-cocycle c with c(1) = 1, c(σ) ∈ G(k(i)) and c(σ) · σ c(σ) = 1. The GK -torsor Isom⊗ K (K ⊗ ω1 , K ⊗ ω2 ) corresponds to an element 1 η ∈ H ({1, σ}, G(K(i))). This element is the image of ξ under the map, induced by the inclusion G(k(i)) ⊂ G(K(i)), from H 1 ({1, σ}, G(k(i))) to H 1 ({1, σ}, G(K(i))) (we note that Gal(K(i)/K) = Gal(k(i)/k)). Since Lj is real, the torsor Isom⊗ K (K ⊗ωj , ρ) is trivial for j = 1, 2, by Theorem 1.5. Thus there exists isomorphisms αj : K ⊗ ωj → ρ for j = 1, 2. The isomorphism α2−1 ◦ α1 : K ⊗ ω1 → K ⊗ ω2 implies that η is trivial. In particular, there is an element h ∈ G(K(i))) such that c(σ) = h−1 σ(h). There exists a finitely generated k-algebra B ⊂ K with h ∈ G(B(i)). Since B is real and k is real closed, there exists, by the real Nullstellensatz, a k-linear homomorphism φ : B → k with φ(1) = 1. Applying φ to the identity c(σ) = h−1 σ(h) one obtains c(σ) = φ(h)−1 σ(φ(h)). Thus c is a trivial 1-cocycle and there is an isomorphism ω1 → ω2 . Hence L1 and L2 are isomorphic as differential field extensions of K. Remark. The natural map H 1 ({1, σ}, G(k(i))) → H 1 ({1, σ}, G(K(i))) is injective, by the above argument. ✷  3  Comments and Examples  The proof of the unicity of a real Picard–Vessiot field uses almost exclusively properties of Tannakian categories. This implies that the proof remains valid for other types of equations, such as: (a). linear partial differential equations, like ∂z∂ j Y = Aj Y for j = 1, . . . , n, (b). linear ordinary difference equations, like Y (z + 1) = AY (z), (c). linear q-difference equations with q ∈ R∗ , like Y (qz) = AY (z). For case (a), the existence of a real Picard-Vessiot field has been proved in [CH]. The proof of the uniqueness result (Theorem 1.1) for real differential 6  fields with real closed field of constants can probably be rephrased for the case of differential modules over a formally p-adic differential field with a p-adically closed field of constants of the same rank. Observations 3.1 Let K be a real closed differential field with field of constants k, M/K a differential module and ω :< M >⊗ → vect(k) a fibre functor. Let L be the Picard–Vessiot field corresponding to ω and G the group of the differential automorphisms of L/K. Let H be the differential Galois group of K(i) ⊗ M over K(i). We recall that G is a form of H over the field k(i). Using the identification k(i) ×k G = H, one obtains on H and on Aut(H) a structure of algebraic group over k. Let {1, σ} be the Galois group of k(i)/k. Then H 1 ({1, σ}, Aut(H)) has a natural bijection to the set of forms of H over k. Although the action of σ on Aut(H) depends on G, this set does not depend on the choice of G. Let η :< M >⊗ → vect(k) be another fibre functor. Then η is mapped, according to Proposition 1.4, to an element in ξ(η) ∈ H 1 ({1, σ}, G(k(i))) (and this induces a bijection between η’s and elements in this cohomology set). A 1-cocycle c for the group {1, σ} has the form c(1) = 1, c(σ) = a and a should satisfy a · σ(a) = 1 (and is thus determined by a). A 1-cocycle for ξ(η) can be made as follows. The fibre functor η corresponds to a Picard–Vessiot field Lη . Both L(i) and Lη (i) are Picard–Vessiot fields for K(i) ⊗M over K(i). Thus there exists a K(i)-linear differential isomorphism φ : L(i) → Lη (i). On the field L(i) we write τ for the conjugation given by τ (i) = −i and τ is the identity on L. The similar conjugation on Lη (i) is denoted by τη . Now τη ◦ φ ◦ τ : L(i) → Lη (i) is another K(i)-linear differential isomorphism. A 1-cocycle c for ξ(η) is now c(σ) = φ−1 ◦ τη ◦ φ ◦ τ . Let Gη denote the group of the K-linear differential automorphism of Lη . The group Gη is a form of G and produces an element in H 1 ({1, σ}, Aut(H)) with H = k(i) × G. We want to compute a 1-cocycle C for this element. Define the isomorphism ψ : k(i) × G → k(i) × Gη of algebraic groups over k(i), by ψ(g) = φ ◦ g ◦ φ−1 . Define τG , the ‘conjugation’ on k(i) × G, by the formula τG (g) = τ ◦ g ◦ τ for the elements g ∈ G(k(i)). Let τGη be the similar conjugation on k(i) × Gη . Now τGη ◦ ψ ◦ τG : k(i) × G → k(i) × Gη is another isomorphism between the algebraic groups over k(i). The 1-cocycle C is given by C(σ) = ψ −1 ◦ τGη ◦ ψ ◦ τG . One observes that C(σ)(g) = c(σ)gc(σ)−1. 7  The map, which associates to h ∈ G(k(i)), the automorphism g 7→ hgh−1 of G, induces a map H 1({1, σ}, G(k(i))) → H 1 ({1, σ}, G/Z(G)(k(i))) → ˜ H 1 ({1, σ}, Aut(H)), denoted by ξ(η) 7→ ξ(η). The forms corresponding to 1 elements in the image of H ({1, σ}, G/Z(G)(k(i))) → H 1 ({1, σ}, Aut(H)) are called ‘inner forms of G’. By §1, η induces a Picard–Vessiot field and a form G(η) of H. Above we have verified (see [B] for a similar computation) ˜ that G(η) is the inner form of G corresponding to the element ξ(η). For the delicate theory of forms we refer to the informal manuscript [B] and the standard text [Sp]. ✷ Examples. We continue with the notation and assumptions of Observations (3.1). (1). Let M/K, ω, L, G be such that G = SLn,k . Since H 1 ({1, σ}, SLn (k(i)) is trivial, L is the unique Picard–Vessiot field and is a real field (because a real Picard–Vessiot field exists). The group SLn has non trivial forms. For instance, SU(2) is an inner form of SL2,R . There are examples, according to Proposition 3.2 below, of differential modules M/K having a real Picard–Vessiot field L with group of differential automorphisms of L/K equal to SU(2). From [Sp], 12.3.7 and 12.3.9 one concludes that H 1 ({1, σ}, SU(2)(C)) is trivial. Again L is the only Picard–Vessiot field. (2). If G is the symplectic group Sp2n,k , then there are no forms and H 1 ({1, σ}, G(k(i))) is trivial. Therefore there is only one Picard–Vessiot field L and this is a real field. (3). Consider a k-form G of SO(n)k with odd n ≥ 3. The center Z of G consists of the scalar matrices of order n, thus Z is the group µn,k of the nth roots of unity. Since n is odd, one has Z(k) = {1}. Further, again since n is odd, the automorphisms of H = Gk(i) are interior and Aut(H)(k(i)) = G/Z(k(i)). We claim the following. The natural map H 1 ({1, σ}, G(k(i))) → H 1 ({1, σ}, G/Z(k(i))) is a bijection. Proof. A 1-cocycle c for G/Z(k(i)) is given by c(1) = 1 and c(σ) = a ∈ G/Z(k(i)) with aσ(a) = 1. Choose an A ∈ G(k(i)) which maps to a. Thus Aσ(A) ∈ Z(k(i)) and A commutes with σA. Further σ(Aσ(A)) = σ(A)A = Aσ(A) and thus Aσ(A) ∈ µn (k) = {1}. Therefore C defined by C(1) = 1, C(σ) = A is a 1-cocycle for G(k(i)) and maps to c. Hence the map is surjective. 8  Consider for j = 1, 2 the 1-cocycle Cj for G given by Cj (σ) = Aj . Suppose that the images of Cj as 1-cocycles for G/Z(k(i)) are equivalent. Then there exists B ∈ G(k(i)) such that B −1 A1 σ(B) = xA2 for some element x ∈ Z(k(i)). We may replace B by yB with y ∈ Z(k(i)). Then x is changed into xy −1 σ(y). And the latter is equal to 1 for a suitable y. This proves the injectivity of the map. ✷ We conclude from the above result that there exists a (unique up to isomorphism) fibre functor η :< M >⊗ → vect(k) (or, equivalently, a Picard– Vessiot field) for every form of H = SO(n)k(i) over k.
