arXiv:1803.01188v1 [math.ST] 3 Mar 2018  ESTIMATION AND INFERENCE FOR COVARIANCE AND PRECISION MATRICES OF NON-STATIONARY TIME SERIES By Xiucai Ding∗ , Zhou Zhou† University of Toronto  ∗ †  In this paper, we consider the estimation and inference of the covariance and precision matrices of a rich class of non-stationary time series. Under mild conditions, we propose an efficient method to consistently estimate the underlying high dimensional covariance matrix and its precision assuming that only one time series is available. Based on our estimation procedure, we provide some useful statistics to test the structure of the covariance and precision matrices of the time series. Monte Carlo experiments are designed to verify the finite sample accuracy of our estimation and the statistical power of our tests. We also prove the high dimensional Gaussian approximation for the quadratic form of the non-stationary time series and give a consistent estimator of the large long-run covariance matrix, which are of great interests by themselves.  1. Introduction. Covariance and precision matrices play important roles in many aspects of multivariate and high dimensional statistics including principal component analysis, discriminant analysis, clustering analysis and regression analysis. In the era of big data, estimating the large covariance and precision matrices becomes the fundamental problem in modern statistical studies. During the last two decades, various special cases of the above estimation problems have been studied. One main research line is to assume that we can observe a p × n large data matrix X, where the columns xi ∈ Rp , i = 1, 2, · · · , n are centered i.i.d samples. The starting point of covariance matrix estimation is the sample covariance matrix. However, in the high dimensional setup when p is comparable or larger than n, it is well-known that it is an inconsistent estimator [13, 27, 34]. To overcome the difficulty from high dimensionality, recent researchers usually impose two main structural assumptions in order to consistently estimate the covariance and precision matrices: sparsity structure and factor model structure. Many regularization methods have been developed to exploit the sparsity structural for the estimation of covariance and precision matrices, for a comprehensive review, we refer to [6]. On the other hand, factor models in the high dimensional setting have been used in a range of applications in finance and economics. Under this structural assumption, many thresholding techniques can be used to estimate the covariance and precision matrices [18, 19]. For a comprehensive review on factor model based methods, we refer to [20]. Most recently, researchers have focused on providing an oracle estimator without the structural assumptions, for instance the optimal rotation invariant estimator [4, 15, 26]. Even though the estimators are inconsistent, they are better choices than simply using the sample covariance and precision matrices. Although high dimensional covariance and precision matrices estimation has witnessed unprecedented development, statistical inference of high dimensional and non-stationary time series remains largely untouched so far. It is notable that due to non-stationarity, the covariance and precision matrices are large dimensional matrices. One line of work is to impose the stationary assumption, where the covariance matrices are of banded structure. In this situation, the thresholding and regularization technique can help to find a consistent estimator [38, 39] using only one time series. Another line of the work is to estimate the covariance matrices (possibly dynamic) of high dimensional time series assuming that multiple time series are available [10]. In the present paper, we consider a centered non-stationary time series x1 , · · · , xn ∈ R, with covariance matrix denoted by Γ = (γij )ni,j=1 and assume that only one time series is available. In this situation, none of the techniques used in the aforementioned papers are applicable in our setting since their estimates either require stationarity assumption or multiple copies of random vectors from the underlying dynamics.
