Adaptive nonparametric estimation for compound Poisson processes robust to the discrete-observation scheme  arXiv:1803.09849v1 [math.ST] 27 Mar 2018  Alberto J. Coca  ∗  University of Cambridge† March 28, 2018  Abstract A compound Poisson process whose jump measure and intensity are unknown is observed at finitely many equispaced times and a purely data-driven wavelet-type estimator of the Lévy density ν is constructed through the spectral approach. Assuming minimal tail assumptions, it is shown to estimate ν at the best possible rate of convergence over Besov balls under the losses Lp (R), p ∈ [1, ∞], and robustly to the observation regime (high- and low-frequency). The adaptive estimator is obtained by applying Lepskiĭ’s method and, thus, novel exponential-concentration inequalities are proved including one for the uniform fluctuations of the empirical characteristic function. These are of independent interest, as are the proof-strategies employed to depart from the ubiquitous quadratic structure and to show robustness to the observation scheme without polynomial-moment conditions. Consequently, estimating ν from discrete observations is a non-linear statistical inverse problem and, after linearisation, it is of deconvolution type with the role of the (unknown) error played by the increments themselves. Roughly speaking, the literature can be split into two main blocks depending on the observation regime: the high-frequency scheme, where ∆n → 0 and T := ∆n n → ∞ as n → ∞ and any rates of convergence are given in terms of T ; and, the low-frequency regime, where ∆n is fixed and rates are in terms of n. In the former, part or all of the ill-posedness of the inverse problem vanishes asymptotically and estimators are built in two different ways: directly from small-time expansions such as that obtained by taking the first summand in (1.1) (see [23, 26] for the expansions and [24, 25, 42] and [46], Section 2.2, for estimation); and, from (1.2) and its generalisation given by the Lévy–Khintchine formula together with small-time approximations that simplify it (see [2, 12, 13, 15, 18, 21]). Both have the drawback that ∆n → 0 sufficiently fast is needed. In the low-frequency regime, the ill-posedness has to be dealt with in full and the route to construct estimators is the so-called spectral approach, initiated by Belomestny and Reiß [5], which uses the Lévy–Khintchine representation without any approximations (see [6, 7, 11, 14, 20, 22, 33, 34, 38, 44, 45, 49]). The exceptions to this division are [46], Section 2.3, and [37], where the spectral approach is used in the high-frequency regime with ∆n → 0 sufficiently fast, and [40], which we mention in more detail below. See [4] for a comprehensive account of the existing results in this field. However, in practice only finite samples are available and the statistician must choose an estimator constructed from one of the approaches above. Hence, estimators that can optimally deal with both regimes at once are of key importance and this is a recent topic of investigation in related problems too such as in estimation for discretely observed diffusions (cf. Chorowski [8]). In Chapter 2 in Coca [10] we showed that, in the compound Poisson setting, the spectral route implicitly uses small-time approximations of arbitrary high-order in ∆n corresponding to those considered in Duval [18], Comte, Duval and Genon-Catalot [12] and Duval and Mariucci [21]. 2  Thus, it should provide estimators that deal with the high-frequency regime with no assumptions on how fast ∆n → 0 to the extent that they should also deal with the low-frequency regime. Indeed, without knowledge of λ, herein we build such an estimator, ν̂, that, in addition, is adaptive to an assumed Besov-regularity of ν for all the losses Lp (R), p ∈ [1, ∞], under minimal assumptions; in particular, in the statistically-relevant case p = ∞, and also when p ∈ [2, ∞), we only require a logarithmic-tail assumption. We remark that the rates will be given in terms of the natural quantity Tλ := λT = λ ∆n n, i.e. the expected number of jumps that Y gives in the whole observation interval [0, T ]. For more general Lévy processes observed discretely, the insight carries over and estimators constructed from the spectral approach are minimaxoptimal and robust to the observation interval ∆n : this was first reported in Kappus and Reiß [40], where a non-adaptive minimum-distance estimator was devised; and, it was subsequently remarked in the discussion at end of Section 3 in Kappus [38] and in Remark 6 in Section 3 in Trabs [49], where they estimate functionals of and the Lévy density, respectively, under the losses |·|2 and L∞ (D), D ⊂ R \{0} compact. Nevertheless, the last two authors do not prove adaptivity of their estimators robust to the discrete sampling scheme. Moreover, the literature on adaptive estimation of Lévy densities from low-frequency observations is scarce (cf. Comte and Genon-Catalot [14] and Kappus [38], Section 4) and only considers the L2 (R) loss under polynomial-tail assumptions. Therefore, our results fill in these gaps starting with the simplest Lévy model given by compound Poisson processes. Right before completing this manuscript, the work of Duval and Kappus [20] was released: like us, they construct an estimator of ν from the spectral approach that is adaptive and robust to arbitrary frequencies. Yet, they work under the L2 (R) loss with a fourth-moment condition and their rates are optimal up to logarithmic terms assuming knowledge of λ. Due to these differences, our proof techniques differ significantly too as we argue in the next section. Lastly, we must mention that a third block of literature considers nonparametric estimation from discrete but irregular or random observations (see [39, 3, 16]). Additionally, an alternative to the aforementioned purely frequentist approaches, the Bayesian approach, has recently been applied to make inference on ν or functionals of it: in a periodic and low-frequency setting, Nickl and Söhl [47] prove a Bernstein–von Mises theorem for its distribution, and non-adaptive minimax-optimal contraction rates of the Bayesian posterior under the supremum-norm follow. Previously, Gugushvili, van der Meulen and Spreij [35, 36] built a posterior for the forward operator in (1.1) that adaptively contracts at the optimal rate for the Hellinger distance on R for the low- and high-frequency regimes, respectively; in [36], they report robustness to the observation interval of their approach. The concentration results developed in our proofs allow to construct a posterior for ν that contracts at the optimal rate for Lp (D), p ∈ [1, 2] and D ⊂ R compact, using the constructive-testing approach introduced in Giné and Nickl [31]. Yet, this is out of the scope of the current work and, furthermore, it requires knowledge of the regularity of ν, so the problem of finding a Bayesian posterior for ν that is adaptive on R remains open.  1.3  Adaptive and robust estimation in Lp (R), p ∈ [1, ∞]  To achieve the generality of our results, both in terms of loss functions and robustness under minimal assumptions, the routes taken in our proofs partly depart from those in existing literature on nonparametric density estimation for discretely observed Lévy processes. This new result implies that, for p ∈ {1, ∞}, the non-linear term does not show exponential concentration but polynomial (of arbitrary high order) and, thus, using Talagrand’s inequality is crucial to apply Lepskiĭ’s method. In particular, for p = 1 it dominates over the linear term, a phenomenon not yet reported in related literature.
