We analyze the covertime of a biased random walk on the random graph Gn,p . The walk is biased towrds visiting vertices of low degree and this makes the covertime less than in the unbiased case.  1. Introduction Let G = (V, E) be a connected graph with n vertices and m edges. For v ∈ V , let Cv be the expected time for a simple random walk Wv on G starting at v, to visit every vertex of G. The vertex cover time C(G) of G is defined as C(G) = maxv∈V Cv . The vertex cover time of connected graphs has been extensively studied. It is a classic result of Aleliunas, Karp, Lipton, Lovász and Rackoff [3] that C(G) ≤ 2m(n − 1). It was shown by Feige [9], [10], that for any connected graph 4 3 n . The asymptotic lower bound G, the cover time satisfies (1 − o(1))n log n ≤ C(G) ≤ (1 + o(1)) 27 is obtained by the complete graph Kn . The asymptotic upper bound is obtained by the lollipop graph, which consists of a path of length n/3 joined to a clique of size 2n/3. The facts that the cover time of a simple random walk can be as large as Θ(n3 ) for some classes of graphs, and that it is never o(n log n) for any graph encourages a study of modified random walks whose performance may be better (in order of magnitude), either in general or for specific classes of graphs. If we restrict our attention to reversible random walks then the lower bound cannot be improved. The properties of weighted random walk on an undirected graph are as follows, for more details see [2]. Let p(u, v) denote the probability of moving from vertex u to vertex v in a single step and let π(v) be the steady state distribution for the walk, assuming that it exists. A Markov chain is reversible if π(u)p(u, v) = π(v)p(v, u). Suppose now that each undirected edge e = {u, v} has a positive weight we = wv,u = wu,v . The P transition probability of the associated random walk at v is p(v, u) = wv,u /wv , where wv = u∈N (v) wv,u , and N (v) are the neighbors of v in G. The P stationary distribution of the walk at vertex v is π(v) = wv /w, where w = e∈E we . We can verify this and the fact that weighted walks are always reversible since for any edge e = {u, v}, π(u)p(u, v) = we /w = π(v)p(v, u). As an example, for a simple random walk, we take wu,v = 1, wv = d(v) the degree of vertex v ∈ V , and p(u, v) = 1/d(v); the total weight w = 2m, and so π(v) = d(v)/2m. An O(n2 log n) upper bound on the cover time for any connected n vertex p graph G, was obtained by Ikeda, Kubo, Okumoto, and Yamashita [11] by using a weight wu,v = 1/ d(u)d(v) for edge e = {u, v}. The fact that the above edge weight is multiplicative makes the walk hard to analyze. The use of a simpler but related weight of w(u, v) = 1/ min(d(u), d(v)) = max(1/d(u), 1/d(v)) was studied in [1].
