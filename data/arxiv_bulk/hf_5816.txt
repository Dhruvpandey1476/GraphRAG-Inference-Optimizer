Jorswieck, Senior Member, IEEE  Abstract Recently, cache-enabled heterogeneous cellular networks (C-HetNets) have been emerged as a promising solution for next-generation wireless networks to cope with exponentially increasing demands for high data rate and low latency multimedia services. In this paper, we design both the caching and the delivery policies in the downlink of an orthogonal frequency division multiple access (OFDMA)based C-HetNet which operates in two phases: caching phase and delivery phase. In the caching phase, we propose novel transmission-aware caching policies by coupling the cache placement strategy and physical-layer transmission to exploit the benefits of flexible physical transmission opportunities for access and backhaul links, simultaneously. We specifically apply a joint optimization of cache placement and ergodic radio resource allocation for both the access and backhaul links to extend the flexibility of the cache placement strategies and cover each time period of the delivery phase. Since most of the current research efforts in cache-assisted wireless networks neglected the fair treatment of mobile users (MUs), in this work, we propose two schemes called proportional fairness and min-max fairness schemes. In the proportional fairness scheme, we minimize the total weighted latency of MUs while in the min-max fairness scheme, we minimize the maximal latency of MUs in each cell. In numerical assessments, we investigate the effect of the proposed fairness and transmission-aware caching policies compared to the conventional proactive caching strategies which are only based on the contents popularity and storage capacities. Index Terms– Transmission-aware caching policy, delivery policy, fairness, latency, OFDMA, HetNet. I NTRODUCTION A. Motivation Research results on traffic explosion issue show that most of the global mobile data traffic is due to frequently downloading a few popular contents from data centers [1], [2]. Besides, duplicated transferring contents through the backhaul links wastes more backhaul resources and also increases the latency of mobile users (MUs) [3]–[5]. Therefore, cache-enabled heterogeneous cellular networks (C-HetNets) have been proposed as a potential solution to cope with the backhaul capacity and availability bottleneck by embedding base stations (BSs) with storage capacity [4], [6]. By means of content caching at BSs, popular contents are stored in local caches (such as BSs) before being requested by MUs such that the user demands for the same contents can be locally served without duplicated prefetching from data centers. To this end, the content delivery performance is significantly improved, especially in terms of latency [4], [7], and in terms of backhaul data traffic which reduces the systems delivery cost [3], [5]. Due to the storage capacity limitation of the cache-assisted devices, all contents cannot be cached everywhere, and we should make a decision about ‘which’ content should be stored in the cache of which device. Generally, cache-enabled wireless networks operate in two phases called caching phase and delivery phase [4], [5], [7]–[10]. The caching phase runs at the off-peak time [4], [5], [8] and the resulting cache placement is valid during the time where the popularity distribution information (PDI) of contents remain constant [8], [10]. Note that, if the PDI of contents changes, the cache placement should be performed again. On the other hand, the delivery phase, in which the content requests are served, operates during all the network servicing time with the confined transmission resources [5], [9], [10]. In the delivery phase, the placement of contents is already done, and the requested contents are delivered to MUs using the transmission resources. Note that the PDI changes slowly and the caching phase results would be valid for longer time, during which, the next delivery phase is performed. Since both the cache placement strategies and transmission policies in C-HetNets affect the latency of MUs, content delivery performance analysis and studying the benefits of femtocaching in C-HetNets by coupling cache placement strategy and physical-layer transmission has been attracted in recent research works [2]–[17]. As a matter of fact, cache placement strategy should be carefully designed such that the flexibility of transmission opportunities could be  3  considered among all MUs to have an efficient content delivery in the delivery phase [4], [7], [18]. In other words, to design an efficient cache placement strategy in the caching phase, we not only consider the popularity of contents and the storage capacities, but also to the channel capacities and transmission delays. Motivated by this, we can increase the flexibility of transmission opportunities in the design of an efficient transmission-aware caching policy by jointly optimizing cache placement strategy and radio resource allocation (RRA). Fairness is another important issue which should be considered in all resource allocation strategies [19]–[21]. The concept of fairness is discussed in many contexts such as economics, computer, and telecommunication systems [20], [21]. From the resource allocation perspective, fairness is critical in cases where a set of resources is shared among several individuals/users. Different schemes are developed for wireless networks to guarantee the fairness among MUs [19]–[22]. It should be noted that considering fairness affects both the design of the cache placement strategies [22] and the delivery policies [19], [23]. B. Related Works The recent works studying cache-assisted wireless networks fall into two main categories: 1) devising efficient delivery policies for a given caching policy; 2) designing caching policies to have efficient content delivery. In the first category, some research works investigate the delivery performance and the benefits of different proactive caching policies in cache-aided wireless networks [5], [6], [9]–[13]. By coupling physical-layer transmission and different pre-fetching strategies in a Fog-radio access network (RAN), the authors in [10] study the design of an efficient delivery policy to maximize the delivery rate under fronthaul capacity and per-enhanced remote radio head (RRH) transmit power constraints. In [9], the authors devise a joint RRH selection and subcarrier and transmit power allocation algorithm for the downlink of orthogonal frequency division multiple access (OFDMA)-based cloud-RAN for the delivery phase based on the given caching status at the RRHs. The trade-off between systems throughput and outage probability is investigated in [6], [11], [12]. By assuming a limited capacity for backhaul and interference alignment, the authors in [5] propose several power allocation algorithms for different objectives in a cache-aided smallcell network for a pre-defined caching status. The authors in [13] devise a joint user association and bandwidth allocation for a certain caching status. Since the proposed solution is based on a  4  centralized manner, a software defined scheduler is applied to have a fast and efficient centralized resource management. In the second category, i.e., developing a transmission-aware caching policy, significant research works are published for different schemes [2]–[4], [7], [8], [14]–[17]. By using the Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers approach, the authors in [14] propose a distributed transmission-aware caching policy in mobile cellular networks which is totally based on the backhaul bandwidth consumption and storage capacities. A transmission-aware caching policy is also proposed for a single-cell LTE system with device to device communications in [3] to reduce both access and backhaul traffic subject to storage capacity and delivery deadline constraints, based on the certain requests of users which is not a practical assumption. In [4], the authors propose a cache placement strategy to minimize the average delay of users in CHetNets by assuming an un-limited storage capacity for macro BS (MBS) and fixed channel state information (CSI) of access links. A transmission-aware cooperative caching policy for Fog-RANs is devised in [7] with the aim of minimizing the average delay of users under storage capacity constraint. They also neglect the backhaul delay analysis and the effect of backhaul RRA on the performance of cache placement strategies. Moreover, they assume a fixed signalto-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR) and estimated interferences in RAN which reduces the flexibility and practicality of the proposed cache placement strategy. Fixed CSI and SINR are also considered in [2] and [8] for the design of cache placement policies in C-HetNets to minimize the total latency at users and maximize the received data rate of users, respectively. To consider fairness, some RRA algorithms are proposed for OFDMA-based cellular networks [19], [23]. More specifically, in [19], the authors propose RRA algorithm for the downlink of a cellular OFDMA-based system consisting of multiple BSs to maximize the weighted sum of the minimal user rates subject to per-BS transmit power constraint. In addition, the authors in [22] design a heuristic fairness-aware cooperative caching approach in mobile social networks to improve the data access fairness. Although many existing transmission-aware caching policies in cache-enabled wireless networks efficiently improve the latency of users, they neglect the fair treatment of users which significantly affects the users latency. C. Contributions We consider a multiuser OFDMA-based C-HetNets consisting of a single MBS and multiple femto BSs (FBSs) with finite storage capacities communicating with a data center with limited  5  wireless backhaul links. This network operates in two phases called caching phase and delivery phase. We design both the cache placement and the delivery policies. We assume that in the caching phase, the PDI and channel distribution information (CDI) are known at a software defined central scheduler which is embedded at MBS and is responsible for resource management [13], [16], [24], [25], while the CSI and instantaneous generated requests (IGRs) of MUs are unknown. Moreover, the received PDI and CDI in the caching phase remain constant during the next delivery phase1. In the caching phase, we design a novel transmission and fairness aware caching policy at the off-peak time based on the PDI and CDI by coupling access and backhaul RRA and cache placement policies. The proposed transmission and fairness aware caching policy extends the flexibility of all existing transmission and fairness aware caching policies and cover each time period of the delivery phase, since the RRA is only based on the fixed CDI of joint access and backhaul links and is referred to joint access and backhaul ergodic RRA. The authentication of our proposed cache placement strategy is guaranteed until the PDI and/or the CDI are changed. We assume that MUs periodically request contents in the delivery phase, where the time duration of each time period is much less than the time length of the whole delivery phase. We also suppose a block fading model for access and backhaul channel gains in each time period [15], whereas, the CSI at the beginning of different time periods may be different. To have an efficient delivery policy at each time period, we apply a RRA for instantaneous performance optimization at the beginning of each time period based on the IGRs of MUs, the caching status, and the CSI which are known at the central scheduler. We note that all IGRs of MUs and the CSI in a time period are completely independent from all other time periods and only satisfy the PDI and the CDI, respectively, since the PDI and the CDI remain constant. In addition, we assume that all IGRs of MUs are served within the time period. Hence, the proposed delivery policies for different time periods are completely independent. The main structure of our caching and delivery policies are illustrated in Fig. 1. To guarantee the fairness among MUs, we propose two fairness schemes, called proportional fairness and min-max fairness. In the proportional fairness scheme, the total weighted latency of MUs is minimized, while in the min-max fairness scheme, since the latency of each MU is 1  In this work, we assume that users do not move during the delivery phase. We solve each non-convex and NP-hard optimization problem by using an alternative algorithm with relaxation method to tackle the combinatorial constraints and prove that each of the proposed algorithms converges to a local optimum solution. We also obtain the computational complexity of the proposed cache placement and delivery policies. Simulation results illustrate that our proposed transmission and fairness aware caching policies based on the joint PDI and CDI significantly improves the content delivery latency compared to the traditional proactive caching strategies which are only based on the popularity of contents. Moreover, we investigate the effect of considering different fairness schemes among MUs on the content delivery performance. D. Paper Organization The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. S IMULATION R ESULTS In this section, simulation results are provided to evaluate the performance of the proposed fairness and transmission-aware caching and delivery policies. In the simulation, a single MBS is located at the center of the circular area with radius 500 m and 4 FBSs are positioned with the coverage area of 50 m. The distance between MBS and each FBS is set to be 200 m and adjacent FBSs have maximum equal distances. Moreover, we assume that the distance between MBS and data center is a random variable in [2000 2500] m. In the considered network, each femto-cell b has Ub = 4 MUs and U0 = 7 MUs are associated to MBS. We assume that the small scale fading of the wireless channels are independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) and modeled as Rayleigh distributions. Hence, the channel power gains are exponentially distributed.  27  The mean of exponential distribution of the channel power gains is also assumed to be 0.2. The path-loss exponent of the path-loss model of the channel power gains is assumed to be 2. In this model, the frequency bandwidth of wireless access and backhaul links are WAc = 5 and WBH = 10 MHz, respectively. In addition, the bandwidth of each subcarrier is WS = 312.5 KHz. Therefore, the number of access and backhaul subcarriers are NAc = 16 and NBH = 32, respectively. The power spectral density (PSD) of AWGN noise is set to be −174 dBm/Hz. Without loss of generality, we assume that the IS acts as an AWGN noise with the PSD of −120 dBm/Hz. The maximum allowable transmit power of each FBS, MBS and data center are max set to be Pbmax = 0.5 Watts, ∀b ∈ B/{0}, P0max = 4 Watts, and PDC = 8 Watts, respectively.  The minimum required access data rate of each femto-cell and macro-cell MUs in the network min min is Rb,u = 3 Mbps, ∀b ∈ B/{0}, u ∈ Ub and R0,u = 1 Mbps, ∀u ∈ U0 , respectively. The  delivery phase has a finite time length, where is divided into some time periods with equal time length T = 40 seconds. The total number of contents is supposed to be C = 10. The size of contents are also modeled as Log-normal distribution with µs = 2 and σs2 = 0.1, in terms of Mbit. The popularity of contents are modeled as Zipf distribution with the Zipf parameter ζ1 = 0.5. We assume that our cache sizes are percentages of the total content sizes [10]. The cache size percentage of each FBS and MBS are set to be 0.3 and 0.5, respectively. Without loss of generality, we assume a symmetric fairness in the proportional fairness scheme. The system parameters are summarized in Table II. We compare the performance of the following caching strategies with our caching policies as: •  Cache most popular (CMP): In this proactive strategy, each BS caches the most popular contents until its storage is full [9], [10].  •  Random caching (RC): In this proactive strategy, each BS caches contents randomly until its storage is full. This strategy does not depend on the popularity of contents.  •  No caching (NC): In this case, the storage capacities are equal to zero. Hence, all the requested contents need to be provided from the data center over the backhaul links [3], [9], [10].  On the other hand, in the delivery phase, we apply the proportional fairness and min-max fairness transmission policies. For simplicity, we denote our proportional fairness and min-max fairness schemes by ‘PF’ and ‘MMF’, respectively. VI. C ONCLUSION In this paper, we investigated the design of fairness and transmission-aware caching and delivery policies in the downlink of OFDMA-based HetNets. Hence, we proposed an optimization problem for each phase in proportional fairness and min-max fairness schemes. Specifically, in the proportional fairness scheme, we minimized the total weighted latency of MUs, and in the min-max fairness scheme, we minimized the maximal latency of MUs in each cell. In order to solve each optimization problem, we devised an alternative algorithm and proved that the proposed algorithm converges to a local optimum solution. In the simulation results, we showed that designed caching policy based on jointly optimizing the cache placement strategy and ergodic RRA improves the delivery performance significantly compared to the proactive caching strategies. Moreover, we investigated the effect of considering the fairness among MUs for the design of both the caching and the delivery policies.  32  A PPENDIX A PROOF OF PROPOSITION 1 After solving the optimization problem (11) and finding (ρt1 , βt1 ) at iteration t1 for a fixed (pt1 −1 , γt1 −1 ) from the previous iteration (t1 − 1), we have 0 ≤ D Pr,tot (ρt1 , βt1 , pt1 −1 , γt1 −1 ) ≤ D Pr,tot (ρt1 −1 , pt1 −1 , γt1 −1 , βt1 −1 ),  (39)  which can be derived due to the fact that solving the equivalent problem (15) by using the standard optimization software CVX with the internal solver MOSEK improves the objective function (15a) or the objective remains constant.
