We show that a regular local ring is a filtered inductive limit of regular local rings, essentially of finite type over Z. Key words : Regular Rings, Smooth Morphisms, Regular Morphisms, Discrete Valuation Rings, Projective Modules 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: Primary 13B40, Secondary 14B25,13H05,13C10.  Introduction Let R be a regular ring. It is well known the following question concerning finitely generated projective modules over polynomial R-algebras. Question 1. (Bass-Quillen Conjecture, [2, Problem IX], [18]) Every finitely generated projective module P over a polynomial R-algebra, R[T ], T = (T1 , . . . , Tn ) is extended from R, i.e. P ∼ = R[T ] ⊗R (P/(T )P ). The Bass-Quillen Conjecture (shortly the BQ Conjecture) has positive answers in the following cases i) if dim R ≤ 1 by Quillen and Suslin (see [18], [19]), ii) if R is essentially of finite type over a field by H. Lindel (see [7]), iii) if R is a local ring of unequal characteristic, essentially of finite type over Z, let us say (R, m, k) with p = char k 6∈ m2 by Swan [10]. Swan noticed that it will be useful for the general question to have a positive answer to the following one. Question 2. (Swan [10]) A regular local ring is a filtered inductive limit of regular local rings, essentially of finite type over Z. A partial positive answer is given below. Theorem 3. ([16, Theorem 3.1]) The Swan question has a positive answer for a regular local ring (R, m, k) in the following cases (1) p = char k 6∈ m2 , (2) R contains a field, (3) R is excellent Henselian. Using the above theorem we showed using Lindel’s and Swan’s results that the BQ Conjecture holds for regular local ring containing a field, or regular local rings (R, m, k) with p = char k 6∈ m2 (see [16, Theorem 4.1]). After almost 30 years, we noticed that complete positive answers of the above questions are missing and there a still people interested to have them. 1  The purpose of the present paper is to give a complete positive answer to the Swan Question (see Theorem 14). Unfortunately, this seems to be not very useful for the Bass-Quillen Conjecture (see Remark 15). 1. Discrete Valuation Rings of unequal characteristic. Take ρ to be the map given by Xi → xi , 1 < i ≤ n.  For the next results we need some preparations. A ring morphism u : A → A′ of Noetherian rings has regular fibers if for all prime ideals p ∈ Spec A the ring A′ /pA′ is a regular ring. It has geometrically regular fibers if for all prime ideals p ∈ Spec A and all finite field extensions K of the fraction field of A/p the ring K ⊗A/p A′ /pA′ is regular. A flat morphism of Noetherian rings u is regular if its fibers are geometrically regular. If u is regular of finite type then u is called smooth.
