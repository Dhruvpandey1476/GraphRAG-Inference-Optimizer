Both notions require a bound on a statistical divergence between two probability distributions. One reason for its success is that differential privacy can often be proved compositionally, enabling easy construction of new private algorithms and making formal verification practical; researchers have developed programming languages and other program analysis tools to prove differential privacy [2, 8, 10, 16, 24, 29, 34, 35] (see [9] for a recent survey). Seeking to further improve these composition properties, researchers have recently proposed new relaxations of differential privacy: Rényi differential privacy (RDP) [25] and zero-concentrated differential privacy (zCDP) [12]. Roughly speaking, standard differential privacy requires a bound the magnitude of the random variable measuring the privacy loss, while RDP and zCDP model requires finer bounds on moments of this random variable. These relaxations give more precise bounds on the privacy cost, potentially allowing more accurate algorithms at a given privacy level. Both RDP and zCDP are defined in terms of sophisticated distances on distributions, so-called Rényi divergences [30]. Previously, Barthe and Olmedo [11] developed abstractions for reasoning about a different family of divergences called f -divergences as part of their work on the program logic f pRHL. The semantic foundation of f pRHL is a 2-witness relational lifting approach for f -divergences, which enable relating two distributions while tracking the f -divergence between them. However, this framework is not sufficient to reason about our target properties for two reasons. First, Rényi divergences are not f -divergences.1 Moreover, zCDP is properly described as a supremum  * 1  This is the full version of the paper. For these reasons, we design of relational liftings supporting more general divergences, allowing direct reasoning about RDP and zCDP. Furthermore, 2-witness relational liftings have only been proposed for discrete distributions [4, 6, 11, 26], while many RDP and zCDP algorithms—indeed, the motivating examples of such algorithms— sample from the Gaussian distribution, a continuous distribution over the reals. Handling such distributions presents measure-theoretic challenges. Sato has previously considered a different semantic model for standard differential privacy over continuous distributions using witness-free relational lifting [32] based on a categorical construction called codensity lifting [21], but at this moment it is not clear how to handle general divergences with their method. To solve these difficulties, we extend the semantic model behind f pRHL in two directions. First, we replace the notion of f -divergences with a more general class of divergences, identifying the basic properties needed for reasoning about RDP and zCDP. Then, we adapt the discrete 2-witness liftings to the continuous case. The main challenge is establishing a sequential composition principle. To do this in the continuous case, we extend the structure of 2-witness liftings to a new notion called approximate span-liftings. Finally, we specialize our general model to Rényi divergence and divergences for zCDP, establishing categorical properties needed to build approximate span-liftings. As an extended application, we develop a sound relational program logic that can verify differential privacy, RDP, and zCDP within a single logic, for programs using discrete or continuous sampling. After overviewing the technical challenges (Section 2) and introducing mathematical preliminaries (Section 3), we present our main contributions. We identify a general class of divergences supporting basic properties similar to the ones identified for f -divergences in [11, 23], and we show that our class can model RDP and zCDP (Section 4). We extend the semantic model underlying f pRHL to the continuous case by introducing a novel notion of approximate span-lifting (Section 5). We develop program logic for DP, RDP and zCDP supporting programs using both discrete and continuous random samplings, and show soundness (Section 6). Given a set X, a σ-algebra on X is a collection Σ of subsets of X including the empty set, closed under complements, countable unions, and countable intersections; a measurable space X is a set |X| with a σ-algebra ΣX , called the measurable sets. A countable set X yields the discrete measurable space where all subsets are measurable: ΣX = 2X . A map f : X → Y between measurable spaces is measurable if f−1(A) ∈ ΣX for all A ∈ ΣY . Any subset S of measurable space X forms a subspace where the σ-algebra is given by ΣS = { A ∩ S | A ∈ ΣX }. A measure on a measurable space is a map µ : ΣX → R≥0 ∪ {∞} such that µ(∅) = 0 and µ(∪i Xi ) = P i µ(Xi ) for any countable family of disjoint measurable sets Xi . Measures with µ(X) = 1 are called probability measures, and measures with µ(X) ≤ 1 are called subprobability measures. For any pair of subprobability measures µ1 on X and µ2 on Y, the product measure µ1 ⊗ µ2 of µ1 and µ2 is the unique measure on X × Y satisfying (µ1 ⊗ µ2 )(A × B) = µ1 (A) · µ2 (B). For any measurable space X and element x ∈ X, we write d x for the Dirac measure on X centered at x, defined as d x (A) = 1 if x ∈ A, and d x (A) = 0 otherwise. Measurable spaces and measurable functions form a category Meas; this category has all limits and colimits, and finite products distribute over finite coproducts. For instance, substitutivity is the generalization of the usual notion of data-processing inequality for f -divergences [28, Chapter 2], while functoriality is the special case where the dataprocessing function is deterministic. These two properties are also known in the differential privacy literature as resilience to post-processing [15, Proposition 2.1], in the randomized and deterministic case. Composability corresponds to composition in differential privacy, which states that we can adaptively compose two differentially private mechanisms. Additivity corresponds to a simple instance of composition where the second mechanism does not depend on the result of the first. Continuity is the generalization of the continuity of f -divergences [28, Theorem 16], which approximates divergences of continuous distributions by divergences of discrete distributions. Reflexivity and composability are key properties to give a structure of graded monad. Intuitively, reflexivity gives a unit, and composability gives a (graded) Kleisli lifting. We also need additivity to give a strength of the graded monad, allowing a lifting on real-valued distributions—often available from known results in probability theory—to be converted into a lifting on memory distributions. Barthe and Olmedo also consider f divergences that satisfy a more limited version of composability, called weak composability. Roughly, these composition results only apply when corresponding pairs of distributions have equal weight; the KL-divergence, Hellinger distance, and χ2 divergences only satisfy this weaker version of composability. While we do not detail this extension, our framework can naturally handle weakly composable divergences in the continuous case. A similar approach has also been used by Barthe et al. [5] in the context of an higher order functional language for reasoning about Bayesian inference. Their type system uses a graded monad to reason about f -divergences. The graded monad supports only discrete distributions and is interpreted via a set-theoretic semantics, again using the lifting by Barthe and Olmedo [11].  8.2  Relational liftings for differential privacy  Approximate relational liftings were originally proposed for program logics targeting differential privacy [14]. The first such system used a one-witness definition of lifting [10], which was subsequently refined to several notions of two-witness lifting [11, 6]. Sato [32] developed approximate liftings and a program logic for continuous distribution using witness-free lifting based on a categorical monad lifting [19, 21]. A witness-free relational lifting for differential privacy was introduced in [32]. This can be seen as an application of the general construction of graded relational lifting [20, Section 5] to the Giry monad, using the technique of codensity lifting [21, Section 3.3] instead of >>-lifting. In contrast, there are now numerous systems targeting differential privacy using a wide range of techniques beyond program logics, including dynamic analyses [24], linear [29, 16, 3] and dependent [8] type systems, product programs [7], partial evaluation [34], and constraint-solving [35, 2]; see the recent survey [9] for more details.  9  Conclusion and Future Work  We have developed a framework for reasoning about two relaxations of differential privacy: zero concentrated differential privacy, and Rényi differential privacy. We extended the notion of divergences to a more general class, and to support subprobability measures. Additionally, we have introduced a novel notion of approximate span-lifting supporting these divergences and continuous distributions. One promising direction for future work is to study the moment-accountant composition method [1]. This composition method tracks the moments of the privacy loss random variable, although it does not directly correspond to composition for zCDP or RDP.
