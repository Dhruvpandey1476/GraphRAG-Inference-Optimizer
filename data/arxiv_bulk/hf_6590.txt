arXiv:1802.08409v1 [math.AC] 23 Feb 2018  THE GORENSTEIN PROPERTY AND CORRESPONDENCES BETWEEN TRACE IDEALS AND BIRATIONAL FINITE EXTENSIONS SHIRO GOTO, RYOTARO ISOBE, AND SHINYA KUMASHIRO  Abstract. The behavior of trace ideals and modules is explored in connection with the structure of the base ring and the ambient module. Firstly, over a commutative Noetherian ring, a characterization of a module for which every submodule is a trace module is given. Secondly, over an arbitrary commutative ring, correspondences between three sets, the set of trace ideals, the set of certain stable ideals, and the set of certain birational extensions of the base ring, are studied. The correspondences work very well, if the base ring is a Gorenstein ring of dimension one. As a conclusion, it is shown that with one extremal exception, the surjectivity of some correspondence characterizes the Gorenstein property of the base ring, provided the base ring is a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension one.  1. Introduction Let R be a commutative ring. For R-modules M and X, let τM,X : HomR (M, X) ⊗R M → X denote the R-linear map defined by τM,X (f ⊗ m) = f (m) for all f ∈ HomR (M, X) and m ∈ M. We set τM (X) = Im τM,X . Then, τM (X) is an R-submodule of X, and we say that an R-submodule Y of X is a trace module in X, if Y = τM (X) for some R-module M. When X = R, we call trace modules in R trace ideals of R. One can consult the papers of H. Lindo [11, 12] for recent progress of a general and deep theory of trace ideals and modules. In [8], J. Herzog, T. Hibi, and D. I. Stamate studied the trace of canonical modules, and developed a very interesting theory. The present research was strongly motivated by [11, 12]. One of our starting points is a question posed by Lindo [12] of when every R-submodule of X is a trace module in X. In [12], it is shown that this is the case, when R is a self-injective ring and X = R, and it was also strongly suggested that the converse is also true, if R is a Noetherian ring. The second purpose of the present paper is to study the structure of the set of trace ideals of R. Let I be an ideal of a commutative ring R and suppose that I contains a non-zerodivisor of R. We set XR to be the set of trace ideals of R which contains non-zerodivisor of R, and explore the structure of XR in connection with the structure of the set YR of birational extensions A of R such that aA ⊆ R for some non-zerodivisor a of R. We also consider the set ZR of ideals I such that I 2 = aI for some non-zerodivisor a of R contained in I. We then have the following correspondences between these sets ξ : ZR → YR , ξ(I) = I : I, η : YR → XR , η(A) = R : A, ρ : XR → YR , ρ(I) = I : I. Our purpose is to clarify the structure of the ring R via these correspondences. This approach is originated in [2], where good ideals in Gorenstein local rings are closely studied. Similarly as in [2], the correspondences behave very well in the case where R is a Gorenstein ring of dimension one, and we have η ◦ ρ = 1XR and ρ ◦ η = 1YR , which we shall explain in Section 3 (Theorem 3.5). However, a little different from [2], our main question is when the correspondence, say ρ : XR → YR is a bijection. As is shown in Section 3 (Example 3.9), there is no hope in general when dim R ≥ 2, even if R is a Noetherian integral domain of dimension two. Nevertheless, with one kind of exceptions, the surjectivity of ρ guarantees the Gorenstein property of R, provided (R, m) is a Cohen-Macaulay local ring of dimension one, which we shall prove in Section 4 (Theorem 4.2). As a conclusion, we will show that ρ is a surjection if and only if R is a Gorenstein ring, when R is the semigroup ring of a numerical semigroup over a field.
