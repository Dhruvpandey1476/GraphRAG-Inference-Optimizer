arXiv:1602.08667v4 [math.GR] 3 Nov 2016  PROOF OF SOME PROPERTIES OF TRANSFER USING NONCOMMUTATIVE DETERMINANTS NAOYA YAMAGUCHI  ABSTRACT. A transfer is a group homomorphism from a finite group to an abelian quotient group of a subgroup of the group. In this paper, we explain some of the properties of transfers by using noncommutative determinants. These properties enable us to understand transfers more naturally.  1. INTRODUCTION A transfer is defined by Issai Schur [6] as a group homomorphism from a finite group to the abelianization of a subgroup of the group. In finite group theory, transfers play an important role in transfer theorems. Transfer theorems include, for example, Alperin’s theorem [1, Theorem 4.2], Burnside’s theorem [5, Hauptsatz], and Hall-Wielandt’s theorem [4, Theorem 14.4.2]. On the other hand, Eduard Study defined the determinant of a quaternionic matrix [3]. The Study determinant uses a regular representation from Mat(n, H) to Mat(2n, C), where H is the set of quaternions. Similarly, we define a noncommutative determinant. It is similar to the Dieudonné determinant [2]. Tôru Umeda suggested that a transfer can be derived as a noncommutatvie determinant [7, Footnote 7]. In this paper, we develop his ideas in order to explain the properties of the transfers by using noncommutative determinants. As a result, we can understand the transfers more naturally. Let G be a group, H a subgroup of G of finite index, K a normal subgroup of H, and the quotient group H/K of K in H an abelian group. The transfer of G into H/K is a group homomorphism VG→H/K : G → H/K. The definition of the transfer VG→H/K uses the left (or right) coset representatives of H in G. We can show that a transfer has the following properties. (1) A transfer is a group homomorphism from G to H/K. (2) A transfer is an invariant under change of coset representatives. (3) A transfer by left coset representatives equals a transfer by right coset representatives. Let R be a commutative ring with unity and RG the group algebra of G over P R whose elements are all possible finite sums of the form g∈G xg g, xg ∈ R. The noncommutative determinant uses a left (or right) regular representation from RG to Mat(m, RH), where m is the index of H in G. We can show that the noncommutative determinant has the following properties. Date: November 4, 2016. 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification. Primary 20C05; Secondary 11H60; 15A15.
