VARIATIONAL LIMITS OF k-NN GRAPH-BASED FUNCTIONALS ON DATA CLOUDS  arXiv:1607.00696v2 [math.ST] 20 Feb 2017  NICOLÁS GARCÍA TRILLOS  Abstract. We consider i.i.d. samples x1 , . . . , xn from a measure ν with density supported on a bounded Euclidean domain D ⊆ Rd where d ≥ 3. A graph on the point cloud is obtained by connecting two points if one of them is among the k-nearest neighbors of the other. Our goal is to study consistency of graph based procedures to clustering, classification and dimensionality reduction by studying the variational convergence of the graph total variation associated to such k-NN graph. We prove that provided k := kn scales like n ≫ kn ≫ log(n), then the Γ-convergence of the graph total variation towards an appropriate weighted total variation is guaranteed.  1. Introduction The development of machine learning and data analysis during the last decades has created new research opportunities for mathematicians and statisticians. Tasks like data clustering, data classification, and dimensionality reduction have been approached using methods whose statistical properties are of interest both for theoretical and practical reasons. In this paper we consider a broad class of approaches to such tasks known as graph-based methods, and in particular those that can be posed as variational problems defined on a graph. In general, the set of vertices on the graph is a data cloud Xn := {x1 , . . . , xn } and the graph structure comes from a similarity matrix for the points. Spectral clustering, total variation clustering, and diffusion maps, are all examples of graph-based methods with a variational flavor (see [5, 6, 16, 17, 21, 22, 26]). In the setting where the data points are assumed to be draws from some unknown groundtruth distribution ν supported on Rd , it is standard to use the Euclidean distance to induce the similarity among points in the cloud in such a way that points that are closer together are more similar than those that are further apart. The most popular graph constructions of this type are ε-graphs and k-NN (k nearest neighbors) graphs. For ε-graphs one needs to fix a decreasing kernel η : [0, ∞) → [0, ∞) and a length-scale ε > 0; in the literature the kernel is typically chosen to be a Gaussian kernel. The weight between two points xi and xj then takes the form   |xi − xj | Wij := η . ε k-NN graphs, on the other hand, are obtained by connecting two points with an edge if one of the points is among the k-nearest neighbors of the other. The goal of this paper is to establish rigorous stability results for variational problems defined on k-NN graphs that are closely connected to tasks like data clustering and data classification (see the examples in Section 2 below to get an idea of the possibilities); the variational problems of interest are defined in terms of a graph total variation energy or a graph Dirichlet energy (see Section 2 below for definitions). By establishing the stability of such variational problems we 1  deduce consistency results for a large variety of statistical procedures defined in terms of k-NN graphs. It is important to highlight that in spite of the popularity of k-NN graphs among practitioners and certain advantages that they have over ε-graphs (see [26] for a discussion), the theoretical aspects of graph-based procedures have been mostly analyzed only in the context of ε-graphs. Indeed, asymptotic (as the sample size grows) properties of graph Laplacians have been studied by Belkin and Niyogi [4], Coifman and Lafon [6], Giné and Koltchinskii [13], Hein, Audibert and von Luxburg [15], and Singer [23]. These results deal mostly with pointwise consistency of graph Laplacians. The work of Arias-Castro, Pelletier and Pudlo [1] study the pointwise convergence of Cheeger energy and consequently of total variation, as well as variational convergence when the discrete functional is considered over an admissible set of characteristic functions which satisfy a “regularity” requirement. Spectral convergence of graph Laplacians, (relevant for data clustering) has been studied, among others, by Huang, and Jordan [25], Belkin and Niyogi [3], von Luxburg, Belkin and Bousquet [27], Singer and Wu [24]. Most of the results previously mentioned are obtained using tools from perturbation theory of linear operators. A different set of tools were introduced in [9] and later used in [8, 11, 12]. The notion of Γ-convergence (see [7] and Section 2.1 below) and the introduction of a suitable metric (the T L1 metric; see [9] and Section 2.1 below) allowing to compare objects at the discrete level with objects in the continuum, were crucial tools when establishing consistency results for a large class of balanced graph cuts in [12] and establishing consistency of spectral clustering under different choices of graph Laplacians in [11]. In all these results, sharp convergence rates for ε := εn guaranteeing the consistency were provided; the assumptions on the kernel η were as general as possible. Here we follow closely the framework from [9] and in particular our main results are analogues, in the context of k-NN graphs, of the results in [9] for ε-graphs. 1.1. Outline. We now present the definition of Γ-convergence in the context of a general metric space. This is a notion of convergence for functionals which together with a coercivity assumption, guarantee the stability of minimizers in the limit. A standard reference for Γ-convergence is [7]. Definition 2.13. Let (X , dX ) be a metric space and let Fn : X → [0, ∞] be a sequence of functionals. The sequence {Fn }n∈N Γ-converges with respect to the metric dX to the functional F : X → [0, ∞] as n → ∞ if the following inequalities hold: • Liminf inequality: For every x ∈ X and every sequence {xn }n∈N converging to x, lim inf Fn (xn ) ≥ F (x), n→∞  • Limsup inequality: For every x ∈ X there exists a sequence {xn }n∈N converging to x satisfying lim sup Fn (xn ) ≤ F (x). n→∞  We say that F is the Γ-limit of the sequence of functionals {Fn }n∈N (with respect to the metric dX ). Remark 2.14. A sequence {xn }n∈N ⊆ X like the one appearing in the limsup inequality is said to be a recovery sequence for x. It is straightforward to show that if one can find recovery sequences for all elements in a set X ′ satisfying: (1) For all x ∈ X there exists a sequence {xl }l∈N ⊆ X ′ such that xl → x and F (xl ) → F (x), then one can find recovery sequences for all elements in X ; a set X ′ satisfying (1) above is said to be dense in X with respect to F . This fact follows from a simple diagonal argument (see [7]). We have presented the notion of Γ convergence in this generality because some of the Γ-limits we will consider are taken in the context of the metric space L1 (D) whereas others are taken in the context of the metric space T L1 (D). Also, notice that when the functionals are allowed to be random (as it is the case for the graph total variation), Γ-convergence has to be interpreted as in the Definition 2.11 in [9].
